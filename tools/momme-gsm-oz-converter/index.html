Momme to GSM Converter

Quick answer

Multiply momme by 4.34 to get grams per square metre: 19 momme is 82.5 g/m², 22 momme is 95.5 g/m² and 30 momme is 130 g/m². Divide g/m² by 33.906 for ounces per square yard. Momme is a weight, not a thickness.

How the conversion works

Momme is a weight. The historical definition is the weight in pounds of a piece of silk 45 inches wide and 100 yards long: 45 in × 100 yd is 104.5 m², and one pound is 453.6 g, so 453.6 ÷ 104.5 gives 4.340 g/m² per momme. The name comes from the Japanese monme, a unit of mass fixed at 3.75 g — which is why momme and millimetres share the abbreviation but measure different things entirely.

Because both g/m² and oz/yd² are area weights, the three-way conversion is a single division each way:

  • g/m² = momme × 4.34
  • momme = g/m² ÷ 4.34
  • oz/yd² = g/m² ÷ 33.906

One caveat this converter cannot fix: the arithmetic is exact, but the input is often not. Weight measured on greige cloth includes sericin that degumming will remove, so a greige “19 momme” can finish two to five momme lighter. Ask which state the figure was measured in before you compare two quotations.

Frequently asked questions

How many g/m² is 19 momme silk?

19 momme is 82.5 g/m² (19 × 4.34). It is the standard weight for silk satin and pillowcases: heavy enough to be opaque in a garment, light enough to drape. For comparison, 16 momme is 69.4 g/m² and 22 momme is 95.5 g/m².

Is momme the same as millimetres?

No. Momme is abbreviated mm in the trade, but it measures weight, not thickness — 19 momme silk is not 19 millimetres thick. One momme is 4.340 g/m², defined historically as the weight in pounds of a piece of silk 45 inches wide and 100 yards long.

Why do two suppliers quote different g/m² for the same momme?

Usually because one number is greige and the other is finished. Raw silk contains 25–30% sericin gum, and degumming removes up to 25% of the weight, so a greige cloth quoted at 19 momme can finish closer to 14–15 momme. Always ask which state the weight was measured in.

How do I measure momme without a lab?

Weigh one metre of fabric in grams on a kitchen scale, divide by the width in metres to get g/m², then divide by 4.34. A metre of 137 cm silk weighing 112 g is 112 ÷ 1.37 = 82 g/m², which is 82 ÷ 4.34 ≈ 19 momme.

Is a higher momme always better?

No. Momme is a weight, not a quality grade. Above roughly 25 momme a cloth becomes too dense for summer wear and costs more for no benefit, while 6–12 momme is correct for chiffon, georgette and linings. Match the weight to the weave and the end use.
