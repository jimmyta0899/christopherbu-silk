Silk Order Weight Calculator

Quick answer

Order weight in kilograms is meters × width in meters × grams per square meter ÷ 1000. One meter of 19 momme silk at 140 cm weighs 0.116 kg. Add 10–15% for cutting wastage. Fabric is quoted per meter, but freight, duty and any raw-silk component run on weight. This is the number that converts between them.

Before you send a PO, ask two questions

First: was the momme measured greige or finished? Second: is the width the greige width or the finished width, after shrinkage? A quotation that answers both is comparable; one that answers neither is not, whatever the unit price says.

Weight becomes money here

An order weight is only meaningful next to the state the cloth was weighed in and the price basis the quotation uses.

  • The HS 5002 document setWhere gross and net weight are stated, and which document the customs figure is read from.
  • How much weight degumming takes offSericin is 20 to 30 percent of raw silk by weight, so a roll weighed before and after degumming is not the same number.
  • Momme vs denierWhich number converts to grams per square meter, and which one does not.

How the conversion works

The maths is area times weight. A meter of fabric covers the width in meters times one meter of area, so its mass in grams is width × g/m². Divide by 1,000 for kilograms:

  • g/m² = momme × 4.34
  • kg per meter = width(m) × g/m² ÷ 1000
  • order kg = garments × meters per garment × (1 + wastage) × kg per meter

What the tool will not do: it does not lay out a marker, so it cannot tell you the true minimum consumption for a pattern. That number comes from your pattern room, because it depends on the piece shapes, the size ratio you cut and the direction the pieces must face. The wastage field is where you enter the difference between your marker and the theoretical area.

One more thing worth stating plainly: a greige order does not weigh what a finished order weighs. Raw silk carries sericin gum at 25–30% of its weight, and degumming removes up to a quarter of that, so cloth bought greige arrives heavier and finishes lighter. If the momme on your quotation was measured on greige cloth, the kilograms you receive will exceed this estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does one meter of silk weigh?

It depends on width and weight: one meter of 19 momme silk at 140 cm weighs 82.5 × 1.4 ÷ 1000 = 0.116 kg. The same weight at 114 cm is 0.094 kg. At 22 momme and 140 cm it is 0.134 kg. Width matters as much as momme.

Why does one supplier quote per kilogram and another per meter?

Two different things are being sold. Silk fabric is normally quoted per meter or per yard, because that is how a garment factory cuts it. Raw silk yarn and greige are quoted per kilogram, because yarn is a material rather than a cloth, and freight and duty run on weight. To compare the two, convert the per-meter figure to a weight using this calculator, then check the widths match: a per-kilogram price at 114 cm and one at 140 cm buy different amounts of cloth.

What wastage allowance should I use for silk?

Typically 10–15% for plain weaves cut from a clean roll, and higher against a print, a stripe or a one-way nap, where pieces must be nested in one direction. Dupioni and jacquard with visible slubs or motif repeats usually need the upper end, because matching the repeat consumes extra cloth.

Does this calculator work for greige fabric?

Not directly. Greige silk still carries sericin gum, which is 25–30% of the raw weight, and degumming removes up to 25% of that. A greige order therefore weighs more than the same order at the quoted finished momme. Ask your supplier whether the momme was measured greige or finished.

Can I use this to price an order?

You can use the weight to compare quotations on the same basis, but this is not a price. Fabric is quoted against a specification, and grade, momme, width, color route and quantity all move the number, which is why we publish no unit price.
