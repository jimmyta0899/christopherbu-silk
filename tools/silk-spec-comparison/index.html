Silk Fabric Comparison Chart

Quick answer

Nine mulberry silk weaves range from 6 momme habotai for linings to 30 momme satin for structured gowns. Within one weave momme predicts body and opacity; across weaves it does not, because the construction sets the hand. Satin is lustrous, dupioni slubbed, habotai crisp, georgette sheer.

The comparison most buyers actually need

If the choice is between two weaves at the same weight, the tie is broken by hand and by what the garment has to do: hold a fold (habotai), float (georgette), reflect light (satin), or hold a sculpted shape with visible texture (dupioni). Weight settles the opacity question; weave settles everything else.

How the conversion works

Every number in the table is transcribed from the specification published on that weave's product page. Nothing is estimated: if a product page says 16–19 momme, this table says 16–19 momme.

Two rows deliberately carry no momme range. Raw silk is a yarn measured in denier — 20/22D — and a yarn has no area weight until it is woven. Silk blend has no fixed range because the buyer specifies the fibre ratio, and a blend's momme states total cloth weight rather than pure silk content.

Frequently asked questions

Which silk is best for a wedding dress?

Satin or dupioni, depending on the silhouette. A fluid bias-cut gown wants 19–22 momme satin; a structured, sculptural gown wants 19 momme dupioni, whose slubs hold shape and photograph with visible texture. Jacquard luo suits a gown where the motif is the design.

What is the difference between habotai and georgette?

Both are light plain-family silks, but habotai is crisp and holds a fold, while georgette is woven from highly twisted yarns and has a crinkled, floating drape. Habotai goes into linings and scarves; georgette goes into anything meant to move.

Why does greige silk not list a single momme?

Because greige is a state, not a weave. Every silk passes through it on the way to being dyed and finished, so its weight follows whichever base weave you order. Tell the mill the weave and target weight and the greige is quoted to match.

Can I compare momme across different weaves?

Only loosely. Momme is weight per area, so a 19 momme satin and a 19 momme habotai weigh the same per square metre but behave completely differently — the weave, not the weight, sets the hand. Compare momme within a weave; compare hand across weaves.

Which weave is lightest?

Habotai, from 6 momme (about 26 g/m²), and georgette from 8 momme. Both are lighter than the lightest satin we stock at 10 momme. For comparison, a 30 momme satin is 130 g/m² — five times the weight of a 6 momme habotai.
