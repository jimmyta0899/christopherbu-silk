Denier is not momme

A quotation that mixes them up is worth questioning. 20/22D describes the yarn on the cone; 19 momme describes the woven cloth on the roll. Converting between them needs the weave density, which is why no honest converter offers a direct button.

How the conversion works

There are two opposite ways to number a yarn, and almost every conversion mistake comes from mixing them. Direct systems measure weight per fixed length — a bigger number is a thicker yarn. Indirect systems measure length per fixed weight — a bigger number is a finer yarn.

Denier, dtex and tex are direct. Nm and Ne are indirect. Everything converts cleanly through tex:

  • tex = denier ÷ 9 (denier is g/9,000 m; tex is g/1,000 m)
  • dtex = tex × 10
  • Nm = 1,000 ÷ tex (metres per gram)
  • Ne = 590.5 ÷ tex (840-yard hanks per pound)
  • Nm ≈ Ne × 1.693

The 590.5 constant is the general one used for man-made and blended staple. Pure cotton uses 583.1, which is why a cotton count converted here can differ in the third digit from a cotton mill's figure. For silk filament — which is what this page is for — denier and tex are the numbers that matter, and those are exact.

Constants and basis

  • ISO — ISO 1144 — Textiles: universal system for designating linear density (tex) (2016)
  • ASTM International — ASTM D2260 — Standard tables of conversion factors for yarn numbering systems
  • GB/T 1797-2008 — 生丝 (Raw silk) — the Chinese standard for the 20/22D notation (2008)

Frequently asked questions

What is 20/22 denier in tex and Nm?

20/22 denier covers 20 to 22 denier, so 2.22 to 2.44 tex, or roughly 410 to 450 Nm. Denier divided by 9 gives tex; 1,000 divided by tex gives Nm. The slash notation means a range, not a fraction — the lot averages somewhere inside it.

Why is silk measured in denier and cloth measured in momme?

They answer different questions. Denier weighs yarn (grams per 9,000 m), so it describes the filament you buy on a cone. Momme weighs woven cloth (grams per square metre), so it describes the fabric you buy on a roll. One does not convert into the other without knowing the weave density.

Does a higher denier mean better silk?

No. Denier is thickness, not grade. Finer denier means a finer filament, which lets a mill weave lighter, smoother cloth — but grade (4A, 5A, 6A) is what states evenness and cleanness. A coarse yarn can be top grade, and a fine yarn can be poor.

What does 2/20/22D mean?

Two plies of 20/22 denier yarn twisted together. The effective linear density is doubled: about 42 denier, 4.7 tex, roughly 215 Nm. Ply notation is written ply-count then count — 2/20/22D is two ends, not one end of 42 denier.

Why do Nm and Ne give approximate answers?

Nm and Ne are indirect counts — they measure length per fixed weight, so they depend on moisture regain and twist level as well as thickness. Denier, dtex and tex are direct weight-per-length measures and convert exactly. Treat any filament-to-spun conversion as a comparison, not a specification.
