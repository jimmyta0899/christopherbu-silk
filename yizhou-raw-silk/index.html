Yizhou, Guangxi: the raw silk origin behind the cloth

Yizhou is the largest raw silk producing county in China. It reels 5,463 tonnes of raw silk a year, 13.4% of national output. It has held first place among Chinese counties for sericulture scale for twenty consecutive years (Yizhou District Government, 2023). The local manufacturers we work with draw from this district. What follows is what the official figures actually say. It also covers where the widely repeated versions are wrong. Then it explains why the origin matters to a buyer specifying cloth rather than a story.

Key takeaways

  • Yizhou reels 5,463 tonnes of raw silk a year, 13.4% of Chinese output. Has ranked first among Chinese counties for sericulture scale for twenty consecutive years.
  • Every figure on this page carries two qualifiers: the administrative body (Yizhou district, not Hechi prefecture) and the measure (raw silk output is not cocoon value).
  • Official sources differ between fifteen and sixteen years at number one on raw silk output, so this page states fifteen or more rather than picking one.
  • Reeling is six steps, and the grade is set at cocoon selection, before anything reaches a loom.
  • Origin does not change the specification you should write. It changes whether the supplier can repeat that spec next season.

Four terms this page uses

Raw silk is the filament reeled directly from cocoons and twisted into yarn, before any weaving. Reeling is that first process. Cocoons are softened, the filament is found and joined, and the yarn is wound onto a bobbin. Greige is the cloth that leaves the loom undyed and unfinished. Lot mark is the identifier a reeling run carries into export packing. It is how a certificate is matched to the bale in front of you.

Grading, moisture content and lot marking are set by the national raw silk standard, GB/T 1797-2008 (Standardization Administration of China, 2008). A certificate that cannot be tied to a lot mark is not evidence of anything.

One clarification before the numbers.

“World’s largest raw silk origin” is a fair phrase. It rests on a chain of facts rather than on a single published ranking. China produces the large majority of the world’s mulberry silk, and Yizhou is the top county within China. We state the domestic ranking because it is published and checkable. A supplier who gives you a global percentage without a denominator is guessing.

From cocoon to yarn: six stages

Most sourcing pages describe raw silk as a commodity with a price per kilo. It is a manufactured product with six decision points. Every one of them shows up later as a defect or a clean run on your loom.

01

Cocoon selection

Cocoons are graded before anything else. Double cocoons, thin-shelled cocoons and stained cocoons go to different streams. The double cocoons are what later become dupion yarn, which is why dupion is a sorted grade rather than a defect.

02

Stifling and drying

Heat stops the pupa from developing and drives moisture out of the shell. Moisture control here is the difference between a yarn that keeps its denier through shipping and one that gains weight and loses strength.

03

Cocoon cooking

Hot water softens the sericin gum so the filament can unwind without breaking. Under-cooked cocoons break; over-cooked ones lose cohesion. This is a feel-and-timing skill, not a setpoint.

04

Reeling (filature)

Six to nine filaments are drawn off together and twisted into a single thread. That grouping is what produces 20/22 denier. It is a range rather than an exact figure, because cocoon shells vary and the reeler is continuously adjusting.

05

Rewinding

The reeled silk is transferred to bobbins or books, inspected for slubs and breaks, and graded. Evenness, cleanness and neatness are assessed here: the three measures behind a 4A, 5A or 6A grade.

06

Packing

Yarn is packed into export bales with moisture content controlled below 11% (GB/T 1797-2008), marked by lot so a batch can be traced back to its reeling run.

Why the grade is decided before the yarn is sold

A 6A grade is not a marketing tier. It is the outcome of three measured properties, each assessed on the reeled yarn:

Evenness

Variation in denier along the thread. A yarn that swings between 19 and 24 denier will weave, but the cloth will show it as barré. Horizontal bands that only appear after dyeing, when it is far too late to reject the yarn.

Cleanness

Count of larger defects per unit length. Slugs, bad casts, waste. These become slubs in the finished cloth. In dupioni a slub is the point of the fabric. In a habotai lining it is a reject.

Neatness

Count of smaller imperfections. This is the measure that most often separates 5A from 6A. It is invisible on a swatch until the cloth is dyed dark.

Grade covers the fiber only. It says nothing about momme, which describes woven cloth, and nothing about weave. Those are separate decisions documented in the momme guide and the fabric construction index.

The luxury raw-material claim, stated precisely

The district government’s published account states that Yizhou’s high-grade raw silk supplies the raw material chains of Louis Vuitton and Hermès. That is the claim, and it is a meaningful one. It means the top of the local grade range is good enough for houses whose rejection standards are brutal.

It is also narrower than it is often repeated as. Being an origin for a luxury raw material chain is not the same as being a named supplier to that house. Fiber moves through reeling, trading, weaving and finishing before it reaches an atelier. The brand at the end rarely knows the county. We report the official statement as written and stop there. We do not extend it to brands the source does not name, and we do not claim a direct relationship we cannot evidence.

How Yizhou differs from the regions buyers usually name

Buyers often ask for “Suzhou silk” or “Como silk”. What they mean is a quality level or a finishing standard. These are different links in one chain, and confusing them is how a specification drifts.

Yizhou, Guangxi

Fiber origin

Cocoon growing and reeling. The output here is raw silk yarn and greige cloth, the input to weaving, not the finished fashion fabric.

Suzhou, Jiangsu

Craft and finishing

Embroidery, dyeing, finishing and a long couture tradition. Strong on craft narrative; it is not a raw silk origin in the same sense.

Hangzhou, Zhejiang

Weaving and OEM

High-volume weaving and garment OEM. The place your fabric is likely woven if it is not woven in Guangxi.

Como, Italy

European finishing

Dyeing, finishing and quality grading for European luxury houses. Most of the raw fiber it processes is imported, including from China.

Where we sit in this

We work with local manufacturers in Yizhou rather than describing ourselves as a trader who found a good price. That distinction matters operationally. It is why we can tell you which reeling run a lot came from. It is why we push back when a specification will not survive degumming. And it is why we refuse to quote a construction the mills cannot actually weave at your quantity.

For example, a buyer may ask for a 16 momme charmeuse at 140 cm. The question we can answer is whether the local manufacturers can hold that width and weight in the same run. The second question is what the graded yarn behind it will be. That answer comes from the reeling floor rather than a catalog page.

The other half of our position is that we are a couture house first. Cloth is judged here by what it does on a body under movement and light. That is the standard which produced the Forbidden City jacquard cape and the hand-embroidered Oscars gown. A mill specification sheet will not tell you that a cloth photographs gray under flash. It will not tell you that it creases at the hip after twenty minutes of sitting. We will.

Questions about the origin

Is Yizhou really the world's largest raw silk origin?

Yizhou is the largest raw silk producing county in China, and China produces the large majority of the world's mulberry silk. So the phrase is accurate as a description of the county's position. It is worth being precise about what it rests on: 5,463 tonnes of raw silk a year, 13.4% of national output, and twenty years in a row at number one among Chinese counties for sericulture scale. Those are the verifiable figures. Anyone quoting a global percentage should show the denominator.

What is the difference between Yizhou silk and Suzhou silk?

They sit at different points in the chain. Yizhou grows cocoons and reels raw silk. It is an origin. Suzhou is a weaving, embroidery and finishing center with a long couture tradition. Suzhou buys fiber; Yizhou produces it. A garment described as Suzhou silk is usually describing where it was made or embroidered, not where the filament came from.

Which luxury brands use Yizhou raw silk?

The district government's own published account states that the region's high-grade raw silk supplies the raw material chains of Louis Vuitton and Hermès. We report that as the official statement it is. We do not extend it to brands the source does not name. Being an origin for a luxury house is not the same as being its named supplier. The fiber usually passes through several hands before it reaches a European atelier.

What does 20/22 denier mean?

Denier measures the weight of 9,000 meters of filament. 20/22D means the yarn falls in a band between 20 and 22 denier. It is a range rather than a fixed number, because it is reeled from natural cocoons whose shells vary. It is the standard spec for mulberry raw silk in international trade, and it differs from momme, which describes woven cloth.

Why does raw silk from one region behave differently from another?

Three variables: the mulberry leaf, the climate, and the reeling discipline. Leaf quality drives filament strength and length. Heat and humidity through the growing season affect cocoon shell thickness. Reeling practice decides how much of that potential survives into the yarn. Two regions can run the same silkworm breed and still produce yarn that breaks at different rates.

Can I visit or audit the supply chain?

Buyer factory audits are possible and we publish the checklist we work to. If you are arranging a visit, tell us in advance what you need to see: reeling floor, grading records, lot marking, moisture testing. Not every stage happens at the same site, and some require the mill to schedule around production.

Sources and method

Methodology: every figure on this page is taken from a published government profile. The two qualifiers that travel with it (administrative scope and statistical measure) are stated in the same row. Where official sources disagree, as they do on the number of years at first place for raw silk output, the page states the conservative figure and says so. Reeled silk is a commodity with published standards, so this page cites the standard rather than a supplier's brochure.
