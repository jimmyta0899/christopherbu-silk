Dupioni Silk Price Per Yard: What Drives the Cost

There is no public per-yard price for dupioni silk on this site, and no price list to download. A written quotation states the cost of one order built around one specification: weight, width, finish, colour route, order size and shipping term. Buyers searching for a cost per yard are asking which fields move the total. This is the cost and compliance counterpart to the specification guide, which covers the cloth and its spec sheet. No figure here is a price.

Key takeaways

  • ›No per-yard price list is published; a written quotation prices one specification for one order.
  • ›A quotation settles cost fields (weight, width, finish, order size) and compliance fields (fibre labelling, test methods, certificate scope).
  • ›Tariff rules split woven silk fabrics by silk content and width, so those fields come before a cost.
  • ›Shrinkage, colour fastness and fabric weight each have a published test method, and one weighed swatch does not certify a whole delivery.
  • ›Verify a certificate number with the issuer's own tool and read its scope.

Why there is no public per-yard price

A single per-yard price assumes one cloth with one fixed set of fields. The site publishes dupioni in 16 and 19 momme, in 114 and 140 cm widths, either greige or dyed to order. Each combination uses a different amount of silk, so the written quotation ties the cost to the combination a buyer confirms.

A written quotation is the document that fixes one order's cost against one specification.

Those fields also set the customs classification. In the HTS 5007 table published by the USITC, raw silk falls under heading 5002 while woven silk fabrics fall under 5007, and the woven heading is divided by silk content and by width, with a 127 cm threshold among the splits1. A quotation missing the composition or the width cannot be matched to the right classification, which is part of the landed cost.

ISO 3801 notes that for some fabrics mass per unit length and mass per unit area are related simply by the width, while for others the structure introduces an important distinction between the two2. A weight figure on a quotation therefore has to say what was measured and on what basis.

For example, two quotations naming the same momme but built on different measurement bases cannot be compared until each says what was measured2.

The cloth is a woven silk with an irregular, slubbed surface, supplied greige (undyed) or dyed to order from a standard shade card or a custom colour. That distinction is a cost field, because dyeing adds a processing step the greige route does not carry. The specification guide covers how the yarn and weave produce the slubs.

The fields a written quotation has to fix

Weight in momme and width in centimetres set how much silk an order uses and how many metres the cutting plan needs. The greige or dyed-to-order finish decides whether a dyeing step is added, and the colour route decides how much work sits behind it, since a custom colour needs a lab dip the buyer approves before the bulk lot. Order size, packing and the shipping term set where freight and handling are divided.

Compliance fields sit on the same document. Fibre content labelling means naming every fibre present at 5 percent or more of total fibre weight by its generic name. A shipment into the United States carries a fibre content label, and 16 CFR 303.6 sets out that requirement, with silk among the named generic names3. The Textile Fiber Products Identification Act supplies the definitions of yarn and fabric behind that label and the labelling duty, although it chiefly covers household textile articles and should not be read as a rule for every garment4.

A finished mulberry silk fabric also sits inside a national standards framework. GB/T 15551-2016, Mulberry silk fabrics, is a current Chinese national standard, published on 25 April 2016 and effective from 1 November 20165. This page makes no claim that the goods conform to it.

Finish, colour and the test method behind them

Greige cloth leaves the loom undyed, while a dyed-to-order length adds a dyeing step; the two are different goods even when the base cloth is the same.

A lab dip is the sample a buyer approves before a dyed-to-order bulk lot is coloured.

Colour needs a test method as well as a shade. ISO 105-C06 specifies methods for determining the resistance of the colour of textiles to domestic or commercial laundering6. A dyed-to-order order should state which method the colour fastness will be judged by, and who runs it, before the bulk lot is approved. The standard describes a test, not a grade for a given lot.

A commercial supplier's terms show the shape a written disclaimer takes. Dharma Trading describes greige goods as washed but neither bleached nor optically whitened, its definition of prepared for dyeing, and states that its fabrics are not pre-shrunk and that small slubs and irregularities are natural rather than defects7. Those are a supplier's own terms rather than an authority on measurement; the structure is what a buyer can borrow. State the finish, the shrinkage position and what counts as a natural variation on the quotation.

Dimensional change, swatches and how a buyer accepts a lot

Dimensional change refers to the change in fabric or garment size after washing and drying, the subject of ISO 50778.

Shrinkage is often a guess, and it has a published test method behind it. ISO 5077 specifies a method for determining the dimensional change of fabrics and garments after a defined combination of washing and drying procedures8. The site's own planning figure of around 5 percent for dupioni is a cutting allowance, not a laboratory result from that method. Keep the two separate: state the allowance the pattern needs and the method by which a finished lot would be checked if a dispute arises.

Sample acceptance needs the same care. ASTM D3776 covers the measurement of fabric mass per unit area, and its small-swatch option carries an explicit limit: the results apply to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot9. The quotation should say what the swatch is for, what the inspection report will measure on the bulk lot, and who signs off when the two disagree.

For example, a buyer who weighs one swatch and finds it heavier than expected cannot reject the whole delivery on that number alone, since ASTM D3776 applies small-swatch results to the sample only9.

An order carries a spec sheet, a lab dip for dyed-to-order orders, an inspection report, a packing list and a certificate of origin where required. Name each on the quotation as an acceptance test both sides accept before approval.

Certificates and the scope a buyer has to check

Certificate numbers arrive with the written quotation and the shipping documents, and a number on its own proves little until its scope matches the goods. OEKO-TEX runs the Label Check where a buyer enters a label number to confirm whether a label is official; the numbers are case sensitive10. That is the verification entry point, not a claim that any certificate on this site has been checked through it.

STANDARD 100, the OEKO-TEX label for textiles tested for harmful substances, sets a benchmark that runs from yarn to finished product and divides certified articles into four product classes; Class 2 covers products with direct skin contact11. A buyer reading a certificate should check which class and which production stage it covers, because a certificate scoped to yarn is a different document from one scoped to a finished garment. The standard describes the scheme; it does not say that any cloth on this site is certified.

A transaction certificate is the lot-level document GOTS uses to track certified material through production12.

GOTS is a processing standard for textiles made with certified organic fibres, and it relies on documented tracking of certified materials through production, using transaction certificates and other verification mechanisms where applicable12. The document to ask for is the transaction certificate that covers the specific lot, and the first thing to check is the scope: the fibre, the product and the processor named. Read that scope beside the specification before approving the order.

Consider a certificate scoped to yarn: it documents the yarn stage, so a buyer cannot offer it as the finished-product document the order calls for.

How to request a written quotation for your specification

Ask for paper before asking for a number. Name the fabric, the weight in momme, the width, the finish, the colour route, the order size, the packing and the shipping term, then add the compliance fields: the fibre content line, the test methods for dimensional change and colour fastness, the inspection documents and the certificate scope you expect.

A sample goes out first, and a dyed-to-order enquiry carries a lab dip. Both sides confirm the inspection report and the certificate scope in writing before the order is approved. Certificate numbers come with the quotation and the shipping documents, and a buyer should verify each one through the issuer's own check tool10.

The planning values on this site are its own published fields, checked against current availability before a quotation is issued; the binding figures for one order sit in the signed documents. A buyer who wants the current published numbers can read the site's own published product specification13.

To request a quotation, send the full specification and ask for a written quotation that names every field above.

Is there a published per-yard price for dupioni silk?

No. There is no public per-yard price and no price list to download. A written quotation states the cost for one specification after the buyer names the fields.

What changes the cost from one order to the next?

Weight in momme, width, the greige or dyed-to-order finish, the colour route, order size, documentation, packing and the shipping term. Weight and width set how much silk an order uses; the finish and colour route decide whether a dyeing step is added. Under the HTS, woven silk fabrics are also classified by silk content and width, which is why those fields are written down before a cost is fixed1.

Does the site publish a fixed minimum order?

No fixed minimum order number is published. The written quotation confirms the order size, the scope and the schedule for one order before approval. Raw silk yarn on this site is traded by weight; the metre is the unit for the woven cloth.

Can I get a cost before approving a sample?

A written quotation follows a specification. A sample, a lab dip for a dyed-to-order order and an agreed test method come first, and both sides confirm the inspection report and the certificate scope in writing before approval. Name the test method for colour fastness and dimensional change rather than a vague promise68.

When does a certificate number arrive, and how do I check it?

With the written quotation and the shipping documents. Verify the number through the issuer's own check tool, then read the scope beside the specification10. For an organic claim, ask for the transaction certificate that names the lot and check its scope before you accept it12.

Methodology: How This Page Was Sourced

Every external claim here points to an institutional page fetched and read before use, and each bracketed number leads to its matching entry in Sources and references. The editorial check keeps two kinds of statement apart. Figures such as 16 and 19 momme, 114 and 140 cm widths and shrinkage of around 5 percent are the site's own published product data, so they carry no external citation. Widely repeated conversions such as one momme equalling a fixed grams-per-square-metre value are not stated as fact, because no authoritative source in the verified set defines them. Standards and certification schemes are described as written; this page does not claim that any product here conforms to a standard or holds a certificate. No per-yard price appears on this page.
