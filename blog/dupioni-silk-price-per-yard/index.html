Dupioni Silk Price Per Yard: What Drives the Cost

There is no public per-yard price for dupioni silk on this site, and no price list to download. Cost follows the specification. In practice, a written quotation states the cost for one order after the buyer names the weight, width, finish, colour route, order size and shipping term. Buyers who search for a cost per yard usually want to know which choices change the total. Those choices are the fields on the quotation. This page names them and shows how each one moves the total. It also sets out how to request the document for a specific specification. It is the cost counterpart to the wholesale silk fabric hub, which follows a bulk order from enquiry to shipment.

Key takeaways

  • ›There is no published per-yard price list. A written quotation states the cost for one specification, not a standing rate.
  • ›Cost moves with the fields a written quotation settles, from momme and width to inspection documents and incoterms.
  • ›Standard weights are 16 and 19 momme, with 21 to 22 momme on request, in 114 and 140 cm widths; 19 momme is the most-requested weight for gowns and bridal (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026).
  • ›In practice, planning values are checked before quotation; the final figures for one order sit in the written quotation and the shipping documents (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).
  • ›To get a cost, send a specification and request a written quotation that names each field in writing.

Why there is no public per-yard price

A per-yard price assumes one cloth with one set of fields. Dupioni does not arrive that way. The same weave comes in 16 and 19 momme, with 21 to 22 momme on request, across 114 and 140 cm widths (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Each combination uses a different amount of silk. For that reason, one standing rate would misstate most orders. The written quotation exists to tie the cost to the combination a buyer actually orders. That is the short answer to a dupioni silk price question: the number belongs to a specification, not to a fabric name.

The site publishes planning values, not a rate card. Shrinkage sits around 5 percent and is accounted for in cutting (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). In practice, 1 momme is about 4.3 grams per square metre (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). That places 16 and 19 momme dupioni near 69 to 95 grams per square metre (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Those values help a buyer plan a cut. However, they are checked against current availability before the written quotation (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). The final figures for one order live in that document and the shipping documents (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). As a result, comparing a planning value with a final figure produces a discrepancy that is not really there. Both documents answer different questions, so read them side by side.

Two site facts are worth stating plainly, because buyers sometimes read them as a retail offer. First, raw silk yarn on this site is sold by weight, not by the metre (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Second, there is no universal fixed minimum order quantity (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Neither one is a promise that fabric is sold by any cut length. Instead, they describe how the raw material is traded. They also show how order size is settled. That happens per specification, in writing, on the quotation.

The yarn in that image is the reason a single rate cannot hold. Dupioni is woven from paired-cocoon filaments, so the weft carries thick-and-thin slubs (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). As a result, the amount of silk in a bolt changes with the weight the buyer picks (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). The site names Yizhou as the geographic origin of the silk and describes the cloth as coming from local mills (Christopher Bu, wholesale silk fabric hub, 2026). Dupioni Silk Fabric Wholesale: A Buyer's Spec Guide covers the spec fields from the product side. By contrast, this page stays with what makes the cost move.

The figures come from the published dupioni product page and trade terms page (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026; Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). In short, read it as a map of cost drivers. It states no prices.

Every row is a line a buyer can write down before asking for a number. The figures in the table are the same planning values named above (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026; Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Weight and width set how much silk an order uses

Weight is the first cost driver, because it sets how much fibre sits in a metre of cloth. Momme is the weight unit for silk fabric (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). The site supplies 16 and 19 momme dupioni, with heavier 21 to 22 momme available on request (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). It also records 1 momme as about 4.3 grams per square metre (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). That places 16 and 19 momme near 69 to 95 grams per square metre (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). In practice, the 19 momme weight is the most-requested option for gowns and bridal (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer who picks it buys more silk per square metre than one who picks 16 momme. For that reason, the field belongs on the quotation in writing.

Width works on the same logic from the cutting table. Widths are 114 and 140 cm (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). For example, a structured bodice gives a different yield from a 114 cm bolt than from a 140 cm bolt. As a result, the metres a buyer orders change with the width. Cost follows the metres, and the metres follow the width. The momme and denier guide explains how the two weight units used across the site relate to each other.

Finish and colour route add a processing step

Finish decides whether a processing step is added at all. Greige is silk that has not been dyed, and it is the standard finish on this site (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Dyed to order is cloth coloured after the order, from a standard shade card or a custom colour (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Both greige and dyed cloth are available on all products. A greige length and a bulk lot dyed to one shade are two different goods. For that reason, the finish is a cost field a buyer should fix at the sample stage. A lab dip is the sample a buyer approves before the bulk lot is dyed (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). It is one of the documents that follows a dyed-to-order order (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

Colour route then decides how much work sits behind that dyeing step. A standard shade card shade and a custom colour are different requests. In practice, the custom route needs a lab dip the buyer approves before the bulk lot (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026; Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

Two-tone iridescence is a separate matter, and it does not add a costed service. Warp and weft are often spun in different colours, so the fabric shifts between two tones as it catches light. That shimmer is a property of the weave, not an applied finish (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer cannot order it as an extra, because it comes from the yarn. Minor weave variations belong on the spec too. Traditional high-quality dupioni is hand-woven, which adds irregularity, and machine-woven versions exist (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Also, the slubs are characteristic of a fine silk yarn rather than defects (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Agree in writing on what counts as an acceptable variation before approving the bulk lot.

Order size, documents, packing and incoterms

The remaining fields are commercial, and each one has a line on the quotation. The site publishes no fixed minimum order number. In practice, the written quotation confirms the order size, the applicable scope and the schedule for one order (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). That confirmation happens in writing before approval. For that reason, a buyer should ask for those fields on paper rather than assume a standing figure.

The documents that follow an order are a spec sheet, a lab dip, an inspection report, a packing list and a certificate of origin (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Each one is a check someone has to produce and both sides have to accept. As a result, the document list is a cost line, not small print. Raw silk grading follows GB/T 1797-2008 (Standardization Administration of China, 2008). The certificate check page names the standard and the public verification entry points behind a scope claim.

Shipping terms decide who pays for what. Incoterms are the trade terms that fix who pays for freight and handling (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Samples go by DHL or a designated courier. Bulk ships FOB Nansha, FOB Yantian, FOB Qinzhou or FCA Yizhou, with FCA Yizhou the preferred term (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). The named term sets where the cost passes from seller to buyer. Lead time is 15 to 25 working days for eligible regular bulk orders (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). In practice, that window starts after the sample, specification, deposit and material availability are confirmed (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Payment for bulk is T/T with a 30 percent deposit and 70 percent against the Bill of Lading (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Packing and the bale or carton spec sit on the same document. They change the freight and handling the incoterm divides up.

Shrinkage changes how much length is ordered

Plan the cut around the shrinkage figure, because it changes how much cloth is bought. The site records shrinkage of around 5 percent for dupioni and accounts for it in cutting (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer who orders to a finished garment length should state that allowance in the specification. As a result, the greige or dyed length arriving at the cutting table covers the pattern. Extra length ordered to cover shrinkage is part of the cost of that order.

The 140 cm width, the momme and the shrinkage allowance belong on the same line of the specification. They are planning values, checked against current availability before the written quotation (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). By contrast, the final figures for one order live in the written quotation and the shipping documents. A buyer who treats a planning value as a final one will find a mismatch that is not really there. The trade terms page sets out which figures are which.

What the couture archive shows about cost on the body

Cost is easiest to see on a finished garment, because the cloth has already been cut. The site states that the mulberry silk woven in Yizhou is the base of the couture gowns Christopher Bu designs (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). In that chain, the dupioni cut for Fan Bingbing in 2013 came from that silk base (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The jacquard of the Forbidden City gown she wore to the 2015 Met Gala came from it too (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). So did the Snow Plum Blossom dress Paz Vega wore to the 2018 Oscars (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The site tags that Yizhou attribution to the label's own production records as original data. These are the only three garments the site names in that chain. The claim does not cover every gown, every collection or every season.

For a buyer, each one is a set of fields on a cost sheet. The 2013 dupioni pieces carry their texture in the slubbed weave (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The site records that a 19 momme dupioni skirt holds its A-line instead of collapsing, because the slubbed weave resists the drape that flattens a crepe (Christopher Bu, 2026). The 19 momme figure is a weight field, and it changes how much silk the order uses (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). By contrast, choose 16 momme for the same pattern and the cloth is softer. The silk per square metre changes as well (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). The 114 or 140 cm width then decides how many metres that skirt needs. In addition, the shrinkage allowance of around 5 percent adds length to the order before cutting (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). As a result, a single silhouette is a weight, a width and a cutting allowance written down together.

A jacquard gown is not a dupioni. The palace motif is woven into the cloth on the loom, while the 2013 dupioni pieces carry their texture in the slubbed weave itself (Christopher Bu, Forbidden City jacquard article, 2026). A woven motif is a different weave field. As a result, it sits on a different line of a cost sheet. A buyer who approves a jacquard and a dupioni in one order approves two different amounts of work at the loom. For that reason, the written quotation has to name each cloth.

The plaid suit above is one of the 2013 dupioni pieces, cut from the same greige dupioni the site supplies today (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The Snow Plum Blossom dress is the embroidery counterpart: white mulberry silk with red plum blossoms applied by hand after weaving (Christopher Bu, Oscars gown article, 2026). Hand work after the loom is another field a written quotation has to name. That work is done to the cloth after the cloth is made. Together with the 2013 dupioni pieces and the 2015 jacquard gown, these are the only three garments the site names in that chain (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026).

Greige or dyed to order belongs in the same argument. A greige length and a bulk lot dyed to one shade are two different goods. For that reason, the finish and colour route are cost fields, not afterthoughts (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Two-tone iridescence comes from warp and weft spun in different colours. It is a property of the weave, not an applied finish, so it is not a separate costed service (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). What lands on the quotation is the spec the buyer approves. In short, the archive shows why that spec has to be concrete.

How to request a written quotation for your specification

Ask for paper, and start with the fields. Name the fabric, the weight in momme, the width, the finish, the colour route, the order size, the packing and the incoterm before you ask what it will cost. Each one is a line on the written quotation. As a result, a specification sent in full gets a cost that matches the goods. By contrast, a vague enquiry gets a vague answer.

The sequence is short. A sample goes out by DHL or a designated courier, and a lab dip accompanies a dyed-to-order enquiry (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Both sides confirm the inspection report and the certificate scope in writing before order approval. Then request a written quotation for your specification and read every field against the published list. What a Written Raw Silk Quotation Must Include sets out how to read each field as an acceptance test. The field list sits at the raw silk spec page. The sample request page covers the swatch that should arrive with the quotation.

Certificate numbers arrive with the written quotation and the shipping documents, not before (Christopher Bu, the certificate check page, 2026). A buyer can then check each scope against the public entry points: the OEKO-TEX label check and the GOTS public licence database (OEKO-TEX Association, retrieved 2026-11-09; Global Organic Textile Standard, retrieved 2026-11-09). Raw silk grading follows GB/T 1797-2008 (Standardization Administration of China, 2008). However, the number alone proves nothing until its scope matches the goods. For that reason, read the scope beside the spec.

Keep the written quotation, the spec sheet and the signed order documents together. The trade terms page carries the schedule. In practice, the binding figures for one order sit in the signed documents (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). As a result, a written quotation that names every field is the document a buyer can cost and sign.

Is there a published per-yard price for dupioni silk?

No. There is no public per-yard price and no price list to download. Instead, the written quotation states the cost for one specification after the buyer names the fields (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

What changes the cost from one order to the next?

The fields on the quotation: weight in momme, width, greige or dyed to order, colour route, order size, inspection and documentation, packing and incoterms, and the shrinkage allowance. Standard weights are 16 and 19 momme in 114 and 140 cm widths (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026).

Does the site publish a fixed minimum order?

No fixed minimum order number is published. Instead, the written quotation confirms the order size, scope and schedule for one order before approval (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Raw silk yarn on this site is sold by weight, not by the metre; that is a statement about the raw material, not a fabric cut-length offer (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

Can I get a cost before approving a sample?

A written quotation follows a specification. A sample, a lab dip for a dyed-to-order order and an agreed test method come first (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). In addition, both sides confirm the inspection report and certificate scope in writing before order approval (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

When does a certificate number arrive?

With the written quotation and the shipping documents. Both sides confirm certification in writing before order approval, within each document's listed scope (Christopher Bu, the certificate check page, 2026).
