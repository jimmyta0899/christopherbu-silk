Dupioni Silk Fabric Wholesale: A Buyer's Spec Guide

Dupioni silk fabric for wholesale is specified on a written quotation. The buyer fixes four fields before production: weight in momme, width in centimetres, finish and colour route. That document turns an approved sample into conforming bulk goods. This page sets out the options the site publishes for each field, the trade terms behind a bulk order, and how to check a certificate claim before approval. It is the commercial counterpart to the wholesale silk fabric hub, which follows a bulk order from enquiry to shipment.

The site's own couture archive shows why the fabric has to be named. The mulberry silk woven in Yizhou is the base of the couture gowns Christopher Bu designs. The dupioni cut for Fan Bingbing in 2013 came from that silk base, as did the jacquard of the Forbidden City gown she wore to the 2015 Met Gala and the Snow Plum Blossom dress Paz Vega wore to the 2018 Oscars (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). One origin produced three cloths. A buyer who writes only "Yizhou silk" on an order has named a place and left the fabric undecided.

What a wholesale dupioni order actually specifies

Start with the fabric, then the numbers. A written quotation for dupioni names the cloth, its weight, its width and its finish. Those four fields decide what arrives at the cutting table. The grade of the raw silk behind the yarn is an earlier decision that the raw silk spec sheet covers separately.

Dupioni is a plain-weave mulberry silk woven from paired-cocoon filaments. Two entangled cocoons are reeled together, so the weft yarn carries thick-and-thin slubs along its length. Woven plain, those slubs become a fine irregular rib that catches light unevenly (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The name appears in several spellings across the trade, among them dupioni, douppioni, doupion, dupion and doppione. All of them refer to the same fabric.

Momme is the weight unit for silk fabric. The site records 1 momme as about 4.3 grams per square metre, which places 16 and 19 momme dupioni near 69 to 95 grams per square metre (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer should state the momme on the order rather than describe the cloth as heavy or light.

Greige is silk that has not been dyed. Greige is the standard finish on this site, and dyed cloth is made to order. A greige sample and a bulk lot dyed to one shade are two different goods, so the finish belongs on the quotation in writing. The site names Yizhou as the geographic origin of the silk and describes the cloth as coming from local mills (Christopher Bu, wholesale silk fabric hub, 2026).

Weight and width options for a bulk order

Two weights cover most orders. The site supplies 16 and 19 momme dupioni, with heavier 21 to 22 momme available on request (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). The 19 momme weight is the most-requested option for gowns and bridal. Widths are 114 and 140 cm. For example, a buyer cutting a structured bodice will get a different yield from a 114 cm bolt than from a 140 cm bolt, so the written quotation has to name one.

The site's 2013 dupioni pieces for Fan Bingbing show why weight belongs on a bulk spec. A 19 momme dupioni skirt holds its A-line instead of collapsing, because the slubbed weave resists the drape that flattens a crepe (Christopher Bu, 2026). A buyer who orders 16 momme for the same pattern gets a softer cloth. The number on the quotation decides how the finished garment holds its shape. The momme and denier guide explains how the two weight units relate.

Greige as standard, or dyed to order

Greige is the default; colour is a choice made to order. The site ships dupioni greige (undyed) as standard, or dyed to order from a standard shade card or a custom colour (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Both greige and dyed cloth are available on all products. A buyer who wants a named shade should say so at the sample stage, because a lab dip is one of the documents that follows.

Two-tone iridescence is a separate matter. Warp and weft are often spun in different colours, so the fabric shifts between two tones as it catches light. That shimmer is a property of the weave, not an applied finish (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). It cannot be ordered as a coating, because it comes from the yarn.

Minor weave variations belong on the spec too. Traditional high-quality dupioni is hand-woven, which adds irregularity, and machine-woven versions exist. The slubs and small irregularities are characteristic of a fine silk yarn, not defects (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer should agree with the supplier in writing on what counts as an acceptable variation before approving the bulk lot.

What the couture archive says about naming the cloth

One origin produces more than one cloth, so an order has to name the fabric. The site states that the mulberry silk woven in Yizhou is the base of the couture gowns Christopher Bu designs. It adds that the dupioni cut for Fan Bingbing in 2013 came from this silk base, as did the jacquard of the Forbidden City gown she wore to the 2015 Met Gala and the Snow Plum Blossom dress Paz Vega wore to the 2018 Oscars (Christopher Bu, Yizhou article, 2026). The site tags that Yizhou attribution to the label's own production records as original data.

The gown above is a jacquard, not a dupioni. The palace motif is woven into the cloth on the loom, while the 2013 dupioni pieces carry their texture in the slubbed weave itself (Christopher Bu, Forbidden City jacquard article, 2026). A buyer who writes "Yizhou silk" has named the county and left the weave open. The written quotation has to go further and state fabric, weight, width, finish and colour route.

The Snow Plum Blossom dress is the embroidery counterpart: white mulberry silk with red plum blossoms applied by hand after weaving (Christopher Bu, Oscars gown article, 2026). Together with the 2013 dupioni pieces and the 2015 jacquard gown, these are the only three garments the site names in that chain. The claim does not cover every gown, every collection or every season.

The link between a spec field and a finished gown stays concrete. The site records that a 19 momme dupioni skirt holds its A-line instead of collapsing, because the slubbed weave resists the drape that flattens a crepe (Christopher Bu, 2026). The 19 momme figure on the published product spec is the same number that decides whether that silhouette stands. A quotation that states only "dupioni" does not say which weight was measured on the sample.

Shrinkage and the cutting allowance

Plan the cut around the shrinkage figure. The site records shrinkage of around 5 percent for dupioni and accounts for it in cutting (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). A buyer who orders to a finished garment length should state that allowance in the specification, so the greige or dyed length arriving at the cutting table covers the pattern.

For instance, a 140 cm width and a stated shrinkage allowance belong on the same line as the momme. They are planning values, checked against current availability before the written quotation (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). The final figures for one order live in the written quotation and the shipping documents. Comparing a planning value with a final value produces a discrepancy that is not really there.

Trade terms and documents on a bulk order

The commercial terms sit on published pages, so a buyer can read them before the first enquiry. Samples go by DHL or a designated courier. Bulk ships FOB Nansha, FOB Yantian, FOB Qinzhou or FCA Yizhou, with FCA Yizhou the preferred term (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). The lead time is 15 to 25 working days for eligible regular bulk orders, after the sample, specification, deposit and material availability are confirmed (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

Payment for bulk is T/T with a 30 percent deposit and 70 percent against the Bill of Lading (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). The documents that follow the order are a spec sheet, a lab dip, an inspection report, a packing list and a certificate of origin (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). Read the trade terms page beside the written quotation, because the binding schedule sits in the signed order documents.

The site publishes no fixed minimum order number. The written quotation confirms the order size, the applicable scope and the schedule for one order, in writing, before approval (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026). A buyer should ask for those fields on paper rather than assume a standing figure.

Standards and how to verify a certificate claim

Raw silk grading follows GB/T 1797-2008, and the site names two public entry points for certificate checks (Standardization Administration of China, 2008; the certificate check page, 2026). The OEKO-TEX label check lets a buyer confirm a label and its scope (OEKO-TEX Association, retrieved 2026-11-09). The GOTS public licence database does the same for organic status (Global Organic Textile Standard, retrieved 2026-11-09). The site lists GOTS and OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 Class II against those public verification entry points.

Certificate numbers come with the written quotation and the shipping documents, not before (Christopher Bu, the certificate check page, 2026). A buyer can then fact check each number against the issuing body before approval. The number alone proves nothing until its scope matches the goods. Destination-market compliance works the same way: ask which test report covers a given claim, then check the report against the certificate scope. One claim, one report, one written confirmation.

How to request a written quotation

Ask for paper first. The methodology is plain: treat every field as an acceptance test, and name the fabric, weight, width, finish and colour route before asking for the quotation. A field only earns its place if a buyer can name the document that satisfies it and the moment it must exist. What a written raw silk quotation must include sets out how to read each one, and the published field list sits at the raw silk spec page.

Consider the sequence. A sample arrives by courier with a lab dip and a test method reference. Both sides confirm the inspection report and the certificate scope in writing before order approval. Then request a written quotation for your specification, and read every field against the published list (Christopher Bu, 2026). Keep the written quotation, the spec sheet and the signed order documents together. A quotation that survives that check is a document a buyer can sign.

Is dupioni available dyed, or only greige?

Both. Greige (undyed) is standard, and the cloth is dyed to order from a standard shade card or a custom colour. Both greige and dyed cloth are available on all products (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026).

Which weight should a bridal order specify?

The site records 19 momme as the most-requested weight for gowns and bridal, with 16 momme also standard and 21 to 22 momme available on request (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). State the momme on the written quotation.

Are the slubs and irregularities a defect?

No. Dupioni is woven from uneven double-cocoon threads, so minor variations are characteristic of a fine silk yarn (Christopher Bu, Dupioni Silk product spec, 2026). Agree on acceptable variation in writing before approving the bulk lot.

Does the site publish a fixed minimum order?

No fixed minimum order number is published. The written quotation confirms the order size, scope and schedule for one order before approval (Christopher Bu, trade terms, 2026).

When does a certificate number arrive?

With the written quotation and the shipping documents. Both sides confirm certification in writing before order approval, within each document's listed scope (Christopher Bu, the certificate check page, 2026).
