Dupioni Silk Fabric Wholesale: A Buyer's Spec Guide

A wholesale dupioni order begins with a naming problem. The word covers a family of related cloths, and the trade sometimes applies it to fabrics that are not dupioni at all. The feature that pins the name down is the slub, a thick, irregular place in the weft that crosses the grain and catches light unevenly. Because the slub starts in the yarn and survives weaving, a buyer cannot judge the cloth from a colour name or a weight alone. The cocoon, the yarn and the weave each leave a mark on the delivered fabric, and a specification has to account for them before an order is written.

Confusion usually arrives through the neighbouring terms. "Raw silk" names a yarn, "greige" a cloth fresh off the loom, and Habotai Silk a separate plain-weave product, yet all three turn up in enquiries about dupioni. Each controls a different decision on an order.

Key takeaways

  • ›The slub on dupioni cloth comes from doupion yarn, reeled from a double cocoon spun by two worms; the filament tangles and comes off coarse and uneven.
  • ›The cloth is a plain weave that pairs a fine regular warp with the thick, irregular doupion weft, which gives a crisp hand and a structured drape.
  • ›Habotai Silk is a separate plain-weave product with a crisp hand and a fine, even surface; raw silk names a yarn state and greige a fabric state.
  • ›Greige, undyed, dyed to order, a standard shade card and a custom colour are the delivery states a buyer has to name in writing.
  • ›The site publishes 16 and 19 momme, 114 and 140 cm, and about 5 percent shrinkage as its own product specification.

Where the slubs start: the double cocoon

A double cocoon is spun by two worms working together, so the filament does not unwind smoothly and tangles easily during reeling. Doupion yarn is a coarse, non-uniform yarn reeled from double cocoons1. The FAO silk reeling manual lists crowded mounting conditions, high temperatures, high humidity and mutation among the causes, and it files double cocoons under defective cocoons1.

A museum object record gives the same origin. The London Museum holds a coiled sample of doppiona silk dated 1900 to 1935, and its catalogue note says the silk is formed when the cocoons of two silkworms become entangled, producing a slightly thicker thread with irregularities2. The note records that the woven textile dyes well and is less prone to creasing2, two properties that matter when a buyer orders cloth dyed to order or a garment that has to hold a pressed shape.

CAMEO, the materials database of the Museum of Fine Arts Boston, defines doupion silk as an irregular thread produced when filaments from two or more cocoons are spun together, with entangled filaments forming a rough yarn of uneven thick and thin areas3. Its doupion silk entry lists the spelling variants a buyer meets in quotations: douppioni, dupion, dupioni and doupin3.

From doupion yarn to plain-weave cloth

The yarn defines the cloth. A doupion thread is thicker and less even than a standard silk filament, so when it is used as weft the thick and thin areas survive into the fabric as slubs running across the width3. Merchant specification pages describe the construction as a plain weave with a fine, even warp and the entangled-cocoon yarn in the weft12. The fine warp holds the structure while the irregular weft breaks the surface into short, uneven ribs that catch light at different angles12.

The fabric is usually judged by its hand and drape. A merchant field list records dupioni as slubby in texture with a structured and crisp drape13, and the site's own product description calls the cloth crisp and sculptural. For a buyer, the crisp hand is why the fabric is chosen for shaped garments, and it belongs on a specification next to the weight.

Colour behaviour follows from the weave as well. When warp and weft are spun in different colours, dupioni reads as iridescent and shifts between two tones as it moves9.

Telling dupioni from Habotai Silk, raw silk and greige

A few neighbouring names sit close enough to dupioni that an enquiry can name the wrong one and still sound plausible. Habotai Silk is a separate site product with its own published specification, while raw silk and greige name a yarn state and a fabric state.

Habotai Silk is the site's plain-weave mulberry silk, a crisp cloth that holds its shape for linings, scarves and light shirts. Dupioni's bold slubs come from the double-cocoon weft3, while Habotai Silk has a fine, even surface. The site lists Habotai Silk at 6 to 16 momme in a 114 cm width, supplied greige or dyed to order, next to dupioni's 16 and 19 momme in 114 and 140 cm widths. The CAMEO *Pongee* entry4 uses the trade term for a wider family of irregular-weave silks; the site files its own plain-weave cloth under the official product name, Habotai Silk, and keeps pongee as a synonym.

Raw silk refers to untreated silk reeled directly from the cocoon7, a yarn state. Greige is the unbleached, undyed state of a textile as taken from the loom8, a fabric state. A lot can pass through both, so writing "raw silk" where "greige" is meant leaves the supplier unsure whether the buyer is ordering yarn or cloth.

The chemistry behind those states also sets a limit on what a specification can promise. CAMEO describes raw silk under the microscope as two fibroin strands held together by sericin, a gum removed by boiling the fibres in soapy water or a dilute alkali5. Its sericin entry states that raw silk contains 20-30 percent sericin6. That figure is the share of gum in the raw fibre, not a weight-loss figure for a finished cloth. The same silk entry notes that weighted silk, treated with metallic salts, can carry about 25 to 50 percent salt5, so a weight claim is worth checking against a test method.

The site's archive labels one 2013 lookbook garment as a dupioni piece in the object record. Recording the fabric by name is the discipline a specification needs when the trade swaps these terms9.

What goes on a dupioni spec sheet

A specification sheet is a list of fields a supplier and a buyer can both check. Two merchant pages show the shape of that list1213. One lists material, weight, width, wash care, feel, usage, a disclaimer about colour variation and natural irregularities, and an option to request a sample12. The other lists composition, available widths, available weights, texture, drape, strength, care and a minimum order quantity expressed as a roll length13. A buyer takes the categories and checks each value against a written quotation.

The composition row has a legal side in the United States. 16 CFR 303.6 requires a textile fibre product to name its fibres by their generic names, and silk is one of the names the rule lists14. The FTC statute defines the yarn and fabric the Act covers and sets out the label duty for those products15.

The slub is a defined feature of the cloth, so the disclaimer covering minor variations belongs in the same document as the weight. A sample clause belongs there too, because a swatch is what both sides approve before the bulk lot is cut.

Weight, width and what a swatch can prove

Momme and grams per square metre both describe fabric mass, and a buyer sometimes treats them as interchangeable. ISO 3801:1977 sets out methods for mass per unit length and mass per unit area, and its text notes that for some fabrics the two are related simply by the width while for others the structure introduces an important distinction between them10. The standard measures how mass is determined; it does not define a conversion between momme and grams per square metre10. A quotation should say which measurement was taken and on what width.

A swatch narrows that further. ASTM D3776/D3776M-20 covers the measurement of fabric mass per unit area, traditionally called fabric weight, and includes an option for a small swatch of fabric11. The standard states that results from that option are considered applicable to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot11.

Consider a swatch whose measured weight does not match the bulk roll: the buyer settles the difference against the written quotation, because the swatch alone certifies only itself11.

Delivery states and the published specification

The state the cloth leaves the mill in is a field in its own right. Merriam-Webster's definition of greige as unbleached and undyed, straight from the loom8, is the starting condition for a lot that will be coloured later. A buyer has four options to name in writing: greige or undyed as the base state, dyed to order, a standard shade card, or a custom colour. Each route leads to a different document at the approval stage.

The figures in this section are the site's own published product specification for dupioni. The cloth is published in 16 and 19 momme, with 21 to 22 momme available on request, in widths of 114 and 140 cm. It is supplied greige or dyed to order, with shrinkage of about 5 percent allowed for in cutting. These are the values the site states for its product, and a written quotation confirms which of them apply to a given order.

For example, a 114 cm bolt and a 140 cm bolt give a bodice pattern different yields, so the written quotation has to name one.

The cloth is woven at local mills. A buyer who needs a named shade should settle it at the sample stage, because the greige base and a lot dyed to order are two different goods by the time they reach the cutting table.

Where the slub sets the tolerance

Minor slubs and small weave irregularities come with a double-cocoon thread, and merchant pages carry a disclaimer to that effect beside colour variation1213. The feature that defines the cloth also sets its tolerance, so a specification should say in writing what range of irregularity both sides accept. The weaker double-cocoon yarn noted by Threads Magazine9 is why seam slippage belongs in the cutting plan for a finished garment.

For example, a buyer who approves a swatch without writing down the accepted range of slubs has no basis for rejecting a bulk lot that shows the same irregularities.

Weight, width and shrinkage are the fields that fix the rest of the order. ASTM D3776 states that a small-swatch result applies to the sample and not automatically to the lot11, so the approved swatch has to be tied to the written quotation that records the agreed weight. The site publishes a shrinkage allowance of about 5 percent for dupioni, a planning value that a cutting plan has to carry if a length is ordered to a finished measurement.

Why does dupioni have slubs?

The slubs trace back to a double cocoon spun by two worms. Its filament tangles during reeling and is set aside to make a coarse, uneven doupion yarn13. Woven as weft, those thick and thin areas show on the cloth as slubs across the grain.

Is raw silk the same as greige?

No. Raw silk is untreated silk reeled straight from the cocoon, a yarn state7. Greige is an unbleached, undyed fabric straight off the loom, a cloth state8. A lot can pass through both states, but they are different things to order.

Can the cloth be supplied dyed, or only greige?

Both. The site publishes greige or undyed as the base state and dyed to order as the alternative, with a standard shade card or a custom colour for a named shade. The written quotation records which route applies to the order.

Are the slubs and irregularities a defect?

No. They come from the uneven double-cocoon yarn3 and are a defined feature of the cloth. Merchant pages list a disclaimer for natural irregularities and colour variation1213, and a specification should record the accepted range in writing before the bulk lot is approved.
