The cocoon, the yarn and the weave each leave a mark on the delivered fabric, and a specification has to account for them before an order is written.\n\nConfusion usually arrives through the neighbouring terms. \"Raw silk\" names a yarn, \"greige\" a cloth fresh off the loom, and Habotai Silk a separate plain-weave product, yet all three turn up in enquiries about dupioni. Doupion yarn is a coarse, non-uniform yarn reeled from double cocoons. The FAO silk reeling manual lists crowded mounting conditions, high temperatures, high humidity and mutation among the causes, and it files double cocoons under defective cocoons.\n\nA museum object record gives the same origin. A doupion thread is thicker and less even than a standard silk filament, so when it is used as weft the thick and thin areas survive into the fabric as slubs running across the width. Merchant specification pages describe the construction as a plain weave with a fine, even warp and the entangled-cocoon yarn in the weft. The fine warp holds the structure while the irregular weft breaks the surface into short, uneven ribs that catch light at different angles.\n\nThe fabric is usually judged by its hand and drape. A merchant field list records dupioni as slubby in texture with a structured and crisp drape, and the site's own product description calls the cloth crisp and sculptural. For a buyer, the crisp hand is why the fabric is chosen for shaped garments, and it belongs on a specification next to the weight.\n\nColour behaviour follows from the weave as well. When warp and weft are spun in different colours, dupioni reads as iridescent and shifts between two tones as it moves.\n\nTelling dupioni from Habotai Silk, raw silk and greige\nA few neighbouring names sit close enough to dupioni that an enquiry can name the wrong one and still sound plausible.
