Hand-Embroidered Mulberry Silk on the Oscars Red Carpet: Paz Vega's Christopher Bu Gown

When the Spanish actress Paz Vega walked the 90th Academy Awards red carpet on March 4, 2018, she wore a strapless white gown covered in large red plum blossoms. The blossoms were not printed and not woven in. They were embroidered onto the mulberry silk by hand, one thread at a time. The dress, named Snow Plum Blossom, was the first Chinese-designed gown on an Oscars red carpet, according to the Global Times[1].

Key takeaways

  • ›The gown is white mulberry silk with red plum blossoms applied by hand embroidery, not print and not jacquard.
  • ›**Hand embroidery** is thread laid on the surface of the cloth after it is woven, so the silk base has to stay flat under hundreds of thousands of stitches.
  • ›Named Snow Plum Blossom, it was the first Chinese-designed gown on an Oscars red carpet, and the first couture piece from the Christopher Bu "Forbidden City" series.
  • ›Buyers specifying embroidered silk should confirm the base weight, the embroidery thread, and whether the work is hand or machine done.

What the gown is made of

The dress is a strapless white silk gown with a sweeping train. Red plum blossoms and branches run across the bodice and down the skirt. A black-and-white check lining showed at the hem where the train lifted. Reporters at the event described the same details: the Daily Mail noted the "large red cherry blossoms" and the "black-and-white slim check print" peeking from under the train2, and USA TODAY and the Sydney Morning Herald both carried the gown on their red-carpet coverage45. Two facts matter for anyone who works with silk. The flowers are embroidery, not print, which means the pattern is thread laid on the surface of the cloth after it is woven. And the base is mulberry silk, the long filament reeled from silkworm cocoons. The combination is why the gown reads as both structured and fluid on camera.

Why mulberry silk, and why hand embroidery on top

Mulberry silk is the long, continuous filament reeled from the cocoons of silkworms fed on mulberry leaves. It carries weight well and takes a drape that a printed synthetic cannot match. For embroidery, the base has to do a specific job: it holds still while a needle pushes through it hundreds of thousands of times. A flimsy base puckers. A mulberry silk base, backed and framed, stays flat while the plum blossoms build up on the surface. The embroidery itself is the second decision. Hand embroidery follows the shape of each blossom so the thread direction changes with the petal, which is what gives the flowers their depth. Machine embroidery runs a fixed path and reads flatter. The red on white here is the hand version, and it is the main reason the gown photographs with that relief instead of a printed flatness. For instance, a petal embroidered by hand curves with the flower, while a machine path repeats the same angle across every blossom.

The 2018 Oscars moment

The 90th Academy Awards were held at the Dolby Theatre in Hollywood on March 4, 2018. Paz Vega, known for Spanish and international film work, was photographed arriving in the Christopher Bu gown. The dress earned her a place in multiple red-carpet roundups, including one from Wonderwall that called it "a true piece of fashion art" and praised its "delicate red blossom print"3. The Global Times reported that it was the first time a Chinese designer's dress appeared on the Oscars red carpet, and that Paz Vega's team contacted the designer directly to ask whether any dresses were available for the occasion1. That last word, print, matters. Several outlets called the pattern a print because that is the shorthand for a garment's surface design. It is not accurate here. The blossoms were embroidered, and the distinction is exactly what a silk buyer needs to see before they specify a similar cloth.

Same series, different craft

This gown and the one Fan Bingbing wore to the 2015 Met Gala come from the same Christopher Bu "Forbidden City" couture series. The series was the designer's first haute couture line, and Snow Plum Blossom was the piece from it that reached the Oscars. They share an inspiration in Chinese textile heritage, but the craft is different. Fan Bingbing's dress is a mulberry silk jacquard, where the motif is woven into the cloth on the loom. Paz Vega's is mulberry silk with the motif embroidered onto the surface after weaving. Jacquard puts the pattern in the weave; embroidery puts the pattern on top of it. A buyer asking for one should not be quoted the other.

What to specify when you order embroidered silk

If you are ordering a similar cloth, pin down four things before you commit. First, the base: ask for the mulberry silk weight and width, so you know what the embroidery is going onto. Second, the embroidery thread and stitch, and whether the work is hand or machine, because that changes the price by a wide margin. Third, the finished yardage, since embroidery work is usually priced on the embroidered area and motif count. Fourth, request a swatch or strike-off before bulk, so the embroidery reads the way you expect under your own light. The safest first step is the same as for any silk order: confirm the hand and the embroidery on a sample before you buy meters.
