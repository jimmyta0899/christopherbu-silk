Greige, Degummed and Dyed to Order: Three

Raw silk is a yarn sold by denier, and greige is cloth in the state it leaves the loom, still carrying its gum. Degumming is the bath that takes that gum out, while dyed to order is a delivery route rather than a kind of fabric. One piece of silk passes through all four states, which is why the words get used as if they were interchangeable.

Key takeaways

  • ›Raw Silk Yarn is reeled filament sold by denier, with the gum still on it2.
  • ›Greige Silk Fabric is a state that every weave passes through, rather than a weight or a variety4.
  • ›Sericin is 20 to 30% of the weight of raw silk (CAMEO)9, and degumming removes up to about a quarter of the cloth's weight.
  • ›A greige 22 momme finishes near 16 or 17 momme, so a quotation should say which side of the bath its figure came from.

Four words, four different questions

A quotation that says "raw silk" can mean a cone of yarn or a bolt of undyed cloth, and the two do not arrive in the same unit, on the same document or under the same trade heading. Raw silk that has not been thrown sits at heading 5002 in the US tariff schedule, while woven silk fabrics sit at 5007 1. The animal is the same. The stage is not.

The words answer three separate questions, and keeping them apart removes most of the confusion that appears at the sampling stage. Raw Silk Yarn says what is being sold, and Greige Silk Fabric says what condition the cloth is in. Degumming names the bath that changes that condition, and dyed to order names the route by which color arrives.

Raw Silk Yarn is a yarn, measured in denier

Raw silk is filament reeled straight from the cocoon and left untreated, which is why the older trade names for it are untreated silk and gum silk 2. Each filament is a pair of fibroin strands cemented by sericin, the gum protein that also gives the yarn enough body to unwind at all 3. The gum is not an impurity to be apologized for at this stage, since without it the filament would part on the reel.

Yarn is sold by denier because denier measures the filament, and our 20/22 denier white raw silk runs to GB/T 1797-2008 with moisture held below 11 percent, shipping as natural white or natural dupion. Denier says nothing about how heavy a cloth will be once that yarn has been woven; that number is momme, and momme only exists when there is a fabric to weigh.

Greige Silk Fabric: the state every weave passes through

Greige is, in Merriam-Webster's wording, "being in an unbleached undyed state as taken from a loom", used of textiles 4. Every woven silk passes through that state, so a habotai, a satin and a jacquard all leave the loom as greige before becoming something else. The word entered English from French *grège*, meaning raw or unfinished, in the 1830s, and it also names a beige-gray color, which is where a fair share of search confusion begins.

Because greige names a condition rather than a cloth, it has no single weight. Our Greige Silk Fabric is quoted by base weave, running roughly 8 to 30 momme across weaves at 114 cm or 140 cm. A mill asked for greige without a weave or a target weight will reply with a question instead of a price.

The trade says greige goods for the same thing. The related term PFD, prepared for dyeing, describes cloth that has been washed but neither bleached nor optically whitened, as one US supplier sets out plainly in its fabric notes 5. The two labels sit close together without being identical, since greige is the state off the loom while PFD is a promise about what has been done to the cloth since.

An undyed roll is still a textile fiber product, because the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act covers "any yarn or fabric, whether in the finished or unfinished state" 6. The fiber rules then require the generic name for anything present at 5% or more by weight, with silk named as silk (16 CFR 303.6) 7, and greige does not escape the label by being unfinished.

Degumming is the process that takes weight out

Degumming is the bath itself, the silk boiled in soapy water or a dilute alkali until the sericin lets go 3. The FAO manual describes the same operation for cocoons, boiled in hot water with added alkali to strip the gum 8. What matters is how far the bath is taken.

The fraction that comes out varies with the degree of degumming, and CAMEO records the residue rather than the loss. Hard, or ecru, silk retains 24 to 26% sericin, souple 17 to 18%, and soft silk under 5% (CAMEO's sericin entry) 9. So "degummed" is not a yes or no answer, and a souple cloth and a soft cloth are both degummed without sharing a hand, a weight or an affinity for dye.

One number needs keeping apart from the rest: FAO states that about 11% of sericin is removed when wild silk is degummed (Chapter 8) 8, and that figure belongs to wild silk. Mulberry raw silk carries far more gum, which CAMEO puts at 20 to 30% of its weight 9, so applying the wild silk figure to a mulberry lot understates the weight change by half.

Dyed to order: what has to be approved before bulk

There are three routes to color, and they are not variations on one another. Greige, shipped as it comes off the loom for the buyer or the buyer's finisher to handle. A standard shade card, where the color already exists in the dyehouse range and the decision is which card to name. And a custom color, matched to a submitted swatch, a Pantone reference or a target sample.

What separates them is what has to be approved before bulk: nothing at all for greige, a shade number for the card, and for a custom match a lab dip signed off against the target under a stated light source. A color that matches under office lighting and fails under daylight is not a match. Dyers publish variation warnings for good reason, and the workable control is a physical sample rather than a screen image. Puresilks, for instance, carries a shade and natural-variation disclaimer on its fabric pages and offers a sample of the quality before an order is placed 10.

Dyeing also moves weight and dimension, since a cloth weighed before the dye bath and shipped after it will not give the same reading twice. That is the practical reason a momme figure on a quotation has to say which side of the bath it came from.

What greige looks like when the roll is opened

Greige mulberry cloth is not white: it runs from cream to a warm ivory, and the tint comes from the gum rather than from any dyestuff. Look along the surface and there are fine slubs where the filament thickens, plus the odd darker fleck of cocoon material that survived reeling. On a satin the light would run down the cloth in a sheet, while here it sits in the texture and stops.

Under the hand it feels firm, dry and faintly papery, which is the sericin at work: CAMEO describes it as a viscous, water-soluble protein covering the surface of raw silk fibers 9. While it is still on the cloth, the fabric has a body that vanishes in the first wet finish, and a fold stays where it is put for longer than it will once the gum is out.

This is where the state and the product get confused: Greige Silk Fabric and Satin Silk Fabric are not two stages of one thing. Greige names a condition that any weave passes through, while Satin Silk Fabric names a five-end weave with a high-gloss face and a matt reverse, supplied from 10 to 30 momme at 114 cm or 140 cm. A satin can arrive greige, and ours does, which is why both names can sit in one specification line without contradicting each other.

The contrast that holds is cloth against yarn. A cone of Raw Silk Yarn has no face to inspect and no width to measure, whereas a roll of Greige Silk Fabric has both, plus a weave, a momme and a hand that changes once it is finished.

Why a greige 22 momme finishes near 16 or 17

The arithmetic is short and it explains a great many arguments: sericin is 20 to 30% of the weight of raw silk by CAMEO's figure 9. The momme guide on this site works with 25 to 30% together with a degumming loss of up to about a quarter of the cloth's weight. Take a quarter off 22 momme and the answer is 16.5, which is why a greige 22 lands near 16 or 17 momme once the gum is out.

Two suppliers can therefore both quote 22 and ship cloth of visibly different weight without either of them lying, because one weighed the roll before the bath and the other after it. The question that closes the gap is a single sentence long: was this figure measured on greige or on finished cloth, and what is the tolerance band.

For example, one order line might read "Greige Silk Fabric, 22 momme, 114 cm, undyed", while the next reads "Satin Silk Fabric, 22 momme finished, 140 cm, dyed to order from the standard shade card". The first names a state; the second names a state, a weave and a route, and only the second can be checked against a finished-weight report when the roll arrives.

Measurement has method behind it. Mass per unit area is measured under ISO 3801, which notes that mass per unit length and mass per unit area are not simply interchangeable, because differences in structure can make the two diverge 11. ASTM D3776 measures the same quantity and carries a caveat worth quoting to anyone who waves a swatch: results from a small swatch apply to that swatch, and not necessarily to the lot 12.

Width belongs in the same conversation, since greige runs at 114 cm or 140 cm by weave, and the same momme over a narrower width means less cloth per running meter. A quotation that states momme without width is half a specification.

Color, fastness and shrink: what gets measured

Fastness gets measured, and ISO 105-C06 sets out methods for determining the resistance of color to domestic or commercial laundering 13. A purchase order that names the method and a pass level is worth more than one that asks for "good fastness", so decide which one you need before the dyehouse decides for you.

Dimensional change has its own method, and ISO 5077 covers the determination of dimensional change when a fabric is subjected to a specified combination of washing and drying procedures 14. The same supplier's fabric page states plainly that none of its cloths are pre-shrunk 5, so the number to agree is an allowance rather than an assumption.

Chemistry sits behind both: OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 is a label for textiles tested for harmful substances, with product class 2 covering articles that have direct skin contact (the standard page) 15. Any label number can then be checked against the issuing body's own lookup 16. Organic greige adds a second layer, since GOTS is a processing standard that relies on transaction certificates to track certified material 17. Ask for the certificate number and the scope, not the logo.

For context, China publishes a current national standard for mulberry silk fabrics, GB/T 15551-2016, issued in 2016 and still in force 18. It tells a buyer that there is a settled technical vocabulary for the cloth, though it is not a statement that any particular lot complies.

What to write on the purchase order

Six lines carry most of the risk out of a silk order.

1. The state: greige as it comes off the loom, degummed and undyed, or dyed to order. One of those three, in words.

2. The weave, and the momme measured on the finished side, with a tolerance band rather than a single figure.

3. The width, in centimeters, since momme without width is half a specification.

4. The color route: a shade card number, or a signed lab dip for a custom match, with the light source named.

5. The test methods and pass levels for fastness and dimensional change, by standard number.

6. The label and certificate numbers to be supplied with the shipment, where a claim is being made.

A seventh line is worth adding: the weight of the sample you approved, since a swatch speaks for itself and not for the lot 12. Where a clause stays ambiguous after that, the contact form on this site reaches the team who quotes the cloth.

Is greige silk the same as raw silk?

No. Raw Silk Yarn is the reeled filament, sold by denier, with the gum still on 2. Greige Silk Fabric is cloth woven from that yarn and left undyed and unfinished 4.

Does greige mean the cloth is gray?

It does not: the word came into English from French *grège*, meaning raw or unfinished, and the cloth itself is cream to ivory. Greige also happens to be the name of a beige-gray color, which is the source of the mix-up, and of a good deal of irrelevant search traffic.

Will the momme change after dyeing?

Yes, and after degumming first. Sericin is 20 to 30% of the weight of raw silk 9, and the bath takes up to about a quarter of the cloth's weight, so a greige 22 momme finishes near 16 or 17. A quotation that does not say which side of the bath its figure came from is describing a different roll than the one you will cut.

Can a satin be supplied greige?

Yes. Greige is a state that any weave passes through, and our Satin Silk Fabric is supplied greige or dyed to order. What changes between the two is the finish and the color, not the weave, the width or the base momme band.

How do I know the gum is actually out?

Ask for the weight of the same lot before and after finishing, and for the residual sericin level if the finisher reports one. The scale is documented: hard silk retains 24 to 26%, souple 17 to 18%, soft under 5% 9, and a finish described only as "degummed" tells you that a bath happened, not how far it went.

Why do two quotes for 22 momme differ so much in hand?

Because one figure was measured on greige and the other on finished cloth, and the gap between them is the gum. The same arithmetic that turns 22 into 16 or 17 also explains why cloth of the same nominal weight can feel like two different fabrics across two suppliers.
