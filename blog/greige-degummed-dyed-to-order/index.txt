Raw silk that has not been thrown sits at heading 5002 in the US tariff schedule, while woven silk fabrics sit at 5007 . The animal is the same. The stage is not.\n\nThe words answer three separate questions, and keeping them apart removes most of the confusion that appears at the sampling stage. Raw Silk Yarn says what is being sold, and Greige Silk Fabric says what condition the cloth is in. Degumming names the bath that changes that condition, and dyed to order names the route by which color arrives.\n\nRaw Silk Yarn is a yarn, measured in denier\nRaw silk is filament reeled straight from the cocoon and left untreated, which is why the older trade names for it are untreated silk and gum silk . Each filament is a pair of fibroin strands cemented by sericin, the gum protein that also gives the yarn enough body to unwind at all . The gum is not an impurity to be apologised for at this stage, since without it the filament would part on the reel.\n\nYarn is sold by denier because denier measures the filament, and our 20/22 denier white raw silk runs to GB/T 1797-2008 with moisture held below 11 per cent, shipping as natural white or natural dupion. Denier says nothing about how heavy a cloth will be once that yarn has been woven; that number is momme, and momme only exists when there is a fabric to weigh.\n\nGreige Silk Fabric: the state every weave passes through\nGreige is, in Merriam-Webster's wording, \"being in an unbleached undyed state as taken from a loom\", used of textiles . Every woven silk passes through that state, so a habotai, a satin and a jacquard all leave the loom as greige before becoming something else. The word entered English from French *grège*, meaning raw or unfinished, in the 1830s, and it also names a beige-grey color, which is where a fair share of search confusion begins.\n\nBecause greige names a condition rather than a cloth, it has no single weight. Our Greige Silk Fabric is quoted by base weave, running roughly 8 to 30 momme across weaves at 114 cm or 140 cm. A mill asked for greige without a weave or a target weight will reply with a question instead of a price.\n\nThe trade says greige goods for the same thing. The related term PFD, prepared for dyeing, describes cloth that has been washed but neither bleached nor optically whitened, as one US supplier sets out plainly in its fabric notes . The FAO manual describes the same operation for cocoons, boiled in hot water with added alkali to strip the gum . What matters is how far the bath is taken.\n\nThe fraction that comes out varies with the degree of degumming, and CAMEO records the residue rather than the loss.
