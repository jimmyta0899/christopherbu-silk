How to Read a Silk Fabric Swatch Card

A swatch card describes one physical piece of cloth, and the line most often misread on it is the weight. ASTM D3776/D3776M-20 covers the measurement of fabric mass per unit area and includes an option for a small swatch of fabric1. For that option the standard states that results are considered applicable to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot1. A figure weighed off a small swatch certifies that swatch. Whether the rest of the roll matches is a separate question the card cannot settle on its own, which is why the fields around the number matter as much as the number.

This page reads a card field by field, borrowing the labeling discipline of a museum object record. It separates what a standard measures from what a supplier states about its own goods. Unit conversion between momme and denier is covered on a separate page linked below.

Key takeaways

  • ›Read the card as a record of one specimen. A small-swatch weight applies to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot1.
  • ›A museum object record supplies the field template: Object type, Materials and techniques, Physical description, Dimensions, Accession number and Record URL23.
  • ›Composition, momme, grams per square metre, width in two units, care, shrinkage, MOQ, swatch size and a disclaimer are the rows to check against a written quotation1011.
  • ›The standards describe how mass is measured; no verified source defines a fixed conversion between momme and grams per square metre8.
  • ›Delivery state and colour terms belong in the written agreement: greige, undyed, dyed to order, a standard shade card or a custom colour715.

Read the card as an object record

A textile in a museum is catalogued as an object with fixed fields, and that structure transfers to a swatch card almost directly. An object record is a museum catalogue entry that names an object through fixed fields such as Object type, Materials and techniques, Physical description, Dimensions, Accession number and Record URL23.

The Victoria and Albert Museum holds a record for a length of shot silk, accession 7193(IS)2. Its fields include Object type, Materials and techniques ("Woven silk"), a Brief description, a Physical description, Dimensions ("Length: 348 cm; Width: 55 cm"), Object history, an Accession number and a Record URL2. A second V&A record, a shawl catalogued as O477820, uses the same field structure, adds Bibliographic reference and Collection, and closes with an Accession number and a Record URL3. Each record names the object in fields rather than leaving it to the viewer.

Mapped onto a card, those fields become the fabric name (Object type), the fibre and weave (Materials and techniques) and the hand and surface (Physical description). Width and swatch size in two units (Dimensions), a stable style or lot reference (Accession number) and the supplier's page for the cloth (Record URL) complete the set. The habit worth copying is narrow: state what can be checked, give each value a unit, and keep one identifier that follows the cloth into the quotation. A card carrying only a colour name behaves like an object with no label.

Composition: name the fibre

The composition row names the fibre, and the word silk alone leaves too much open. Cultivated mulberry silk and wild silk are different fibres. Tussah silk is the coarser, stronger, brownish fibre produced by wild silkworms such as Antheraea mylitta, and it is harder to bleach than cultivated silk4. A card that intends mulberry silk should say so, because the two take dye and finishing differently.

In the United States the name also carries a legal duty. Regulation 16 CFR 303.6 requires fibres present at 5 percent or more of the total fibre weight to be named by their generic names5. Silk is one of the names the rule lists5. The FTC's Textile Fiber Products Identification Act defines the yarns and fabrics it covers and requires a label with words and figures that are plainly legible6. The composition row therefore feeds straight into the label the Act requires.

The site's own product fields state composition per item: mulberry silk for its greige and dupioni cloth, and a mulberry silk and spandex blend for its stretch satin. Those are the site's published values, and the written quotation confirms the composition for a given order.

Weight: momme, grams per square metre, and what was weighed

Merchant cards often print both a momme figure and a gram figure. Momme is the unit used to designate the weight of silk, and a higher momme number means a heavier silk7. That is a qualitative reading of the number, not a recipe for turning it into another unit.

The gram figure has a measurement standard behind it. ISO 3801:1977 sets out methods for mass per unit length and mass per unit area8. It notes that for some fabrics the two are related simply by the width, while for others the structure introduces an important distinction between them8. The standard describes how mass is determined; it does not define a conversion between momme and grams per square metre8. A quotation should say which measurement was taken and on what width.

A weight figure also says little on its own about what is in the cloth. Sericin is the gum protein that holds the two fibroin strands of raw silk together, removed by boiling the fibres in soapy water or a dilute alkali9. CAMEO's silk entry also records that weighted silk treated with metallic salts can contain about 25 to 50 percent salt9. A heavier number can reflect added weight rather than more silk, another reason to keep the test method in the written specification.

Then there is the boundary of the measurement itself. ASTM D3776/D3776M-20 covers fabric mass per unit area, traditionally called fabric weight, and includes an option for a small swatch of fabric1. A small-swatch result is a mass-per-unit-area measurement that the standard says applies to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot1. A buyer who approves a swatch has approved that swatch; the weight of the bulk roll is settled against the written quotation. This page gives no fixed coefficient between momme and grams per square metre, because none of the verified sources defines one. The site publishes its own weight figures only as its product specification.

Width, in two units, and the swatch itself

A card prints width in two units, and a buyer should fix one of them in writing. One merchant prints width in inches and centimetres on the same line10; another lists its available widths in inches11. The site publishes 114 cm and 140 cm for several of its cloths as its own specification, so a 114 cm bolt and a 140 cm bolt give a cutting plan different yields. Width is a dimension, and it is also the value that turns a mass-per-unit-length figure into a mass-per-unit-area figure8.

The swatch has dimensions of its own. A merchant page offers a sample of the fabric beside its specification fields10, and ASTM D3776's small-swatch option is precisely about weighing a small piece1. A card should state the swatch size and the width the bulk cloth will arrive in, so the two are not confused when the weight is checked.

Care, shrinkage and colour

Care belongs next to composition, because the two travel together onto a label. One merchant card says dry clean only10; another lists dry cleaning, a cool hand wash, a low iron on the reverse and keeping the cloth out of direct sun11. For a lot that is dyed to order, resistance of the colour to laundering is something ISO 105-C06:2010 provides test methods for13, so the care line and the colour agreement belong in the same written specification.

Shrinkage belongs on the card as a declared value with its conditions attached. Dharma Trading states that its fabrics are not considered pre-shrunk7. Dimensional change is the change in fabric dimensions that ISO 5077:2007 measures under a defined combination of washing and drying procedures12. A percentage is meaningful only when the conditions behind it are named. The site publishes about 5 percent shrinkage for its dupioni as its own planning value to be allowed for in cutting, a cutting allowance rather than a laboratory result.

MOQ, roll length, certificates and the disclaimer

Two rows are easy to overlook and expensive to leave blank. A merchant card expresses its minimum as a roll length11, while the site's own product pages state a reference MOQ with no fixed minimum, confirmed in the written quotation. Both are trade terms, and neither belongs in the fabric description.

If a card prints a certification label, its number can be checked rather than taken on trust. OEKO-TEX runs a Label Check where a label number is entered, and notes that the numbers are case sensitive14. Record the number on the quotation and verify it; a logo alone is not evidence.

The disclaimer covers what the cloth will naturally do. A merchant card pairs its fields with a disclaimer about colour variation, natural irregularities and ordering extra material10. Dharma Trading writes that tiny slubs and similar irregularities are to be expected and are in no way to be considered defects7. Threads Magazine reports that stores sometimes blur one silk for another, labelling bolts with a combined name or using the wrong term outright17. A disclaimer is where both sides record, in writing, the range of irregularity and colour variation they accept before a bulk lot is cut.

Greige, PFD, RTD and the name problem

The state of the cloth is a field, not a variety. Greige is the unbleached, undyed state of a textile as taken from the loom, a word borrowed from the French grège, "raw, unfinished (of silk)"15. Dharma Trading calls its washed, unbleached, unwhitened cloth greige goods7. PFD means prepared for dyeing, the sense Dharma Trading gives for that same uncoloured state7.

A card may show greige, PFD or RTD for the uncoloured base. These labels are used loosely across suppliers, so the written quotation should state what preparation has actually been done715. It should also name one delivery state: greige or undyed, dyed to order, a standard shade card, or a custom colour. The site describes its cloth as woven at local mills and ships it in those states.

The name field is the least reliable row on a card. CAMEO's doupion silk entry lists douppioni, dupion, dupioni and doupin as spellings of the same thread16. Raw silk is untreated silk reeled directly from the cocoon, a yarn state rather than a fabric state18. A single cloth can therefore arrive under several names, and a name that sounds like a fibre can describe a yarn. Record one name, keep its aliases beside it, and do not infer composition, weave or age from it. The fields should carry only what both sides can check.

How a buyer misreads a card

The errors are procedural, and they follow from treating the card as a promise about a lot. For example, if a buyer approves a swatch on its hand alone, the weight on the card has not been tied to the roll that will be cut1. A buyer can contact the supplier to confirm which test method produced a weight figure; if the card never records it, the answer has to be fixed in writing18. If the width is read in only one of the two printed units, a cutting plan can be built on the wrong bolt1011.

For instance, a card that says greige without naming the preparation leaves open whether the cloth was washed, bleached or left as taken from the loom715. Consider a card whose disclaimer is blank. If the accepted range of slubs and colour variation is not written down, a bulk lot showing the same irregularities as the approved swatch gives both sides no agreed test710. If a certification label number is copied into a quotation but never checked, the logo stands in for the verification the Label Check exists to provide14.

In each case the failure sits in the fields, not in the cloth. A reader who records one name, one width, one weight test and one agreed tolerance has a basis for acceptance1. A reader who does not has only the swatch1.

The finish is greige or dyed to order from a standard shade card or a custom colour. A reference MOQ with no fixed minimum is confirmed in the written quotation. Does a swatch card weight guarantee the whole roll?

No. ASTM D3776/D3776M-20 states that a small-swatch result applies to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot1. The bulk weight is settled against the written quotation.

Is momme the same as grams per square metre?

No. ISO 3801:1977 describes how mass per unit area and mass per unit length are measured, and it defines no conversion between momme and grams per square metre8. A quotation should name the measurement and the width.

Do greige, dyed to order and a standard shade card have to be named separately?

Yes. Greige is the unbleached, undyed state as taken from the loom15. Dyed to order and a standard shade card are delivery routes, and the written quotation records which one applies.

Are slubs and colour variation defects?

No. Merchant cards disclaim natural irregularities and colour variation107. Both sides should record the accepted range in writing before a bulk lot is cut.
