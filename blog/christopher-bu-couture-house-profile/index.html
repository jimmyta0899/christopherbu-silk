Douppioni Silk in Practice: From Greige Slub to Exquisite Garments - Fan Bingbing's Story

Douppioni is a mulberry silk with a visible, irregular texture. Two silkworms spin one cocoon, their filaments tangle, and the yarn comes off the reel with thick-and-thin slubs along its length. Woven plain, those slubs give a dress structure and a play of light that a flat crepe cannot match. This guide walks the fabric from raw silk to a finished garment, with the 2013 Fan Bingbing pieces as proof of how it performs on a body.

Key takeaways

  • ›Douppioni forms when two silkworms spin a single cocoon and the filaments cannot separate cleanly, leaving slubs in the yarn.
  • ›We supply it as greige in 19, 23, 30 and 40 momme, in 90, 114 and 140 cm widths; 19 momme is the standard gown weight.
  • ›Allow about 5% shrinkage because the slub is part of the weave, so a cutter builds it into the block.
  • ›Fan Bingbing wore two Christopher Bu douppioni pieces in 2013, one in Paris and one in Cannes, and the photographs show how the cloth holds a shape.

Step 1: raw silk, reeled and wound

Every douppioni starts as raw silk. In Yizhou, Guangxi, mulberry cocoons are reeled into 20/22 denier filature silk, graded 4A to 6A under GB/T 1797-2008, and wound into hanks. This is the same raw silk we supply to weaving mills, and it is the base yarn a douppioni loom feeds on. The quality at this stage sets the ceiling for everything after it. The weights and grades cited here follow the same spec sheet we give buyers, so the numbers match what a mill will quote.

Step 2: the double-cocoon weave that makes the slub

Douppioni is a plain-weave mulberry silk woven from paired-cocoon filaments. Because the two filaments refuse to separate cleanly, the yarn carries thick-and-thin slubs along its length. Woven plain, those slubs become the fabric's signature: a fine irregular rib that reflects light unevenly. The texture is part of the weave, which is why it reads as deliberate and why no two lengths look exactly the same.

Step 3: the weights, widths and shrinkage to specify

We supply douppioni as greige, undyed cloth straight off the loom, so your finishing house or atelier controls the color. Momme is a silk weight unit; one momme equals about 3.75 grams per square meter. Slub is the irregular thick-and-thin texture woven into the cloth. Standard douppioni weights are 19, 23, 30 and 40 momme in 90, 114 and 140 cm widths. The 19 momme douppioni is the entry weight for gowns: light enough to move, heavy enough to hold a silhouette. Because the slub is part of the weave, allow about 5% shrinkage in the cutting block, the hand-wash shrinkage standard for dupioni (Xingye Textile, 2024).

Step 4: the cloth on a body, in 2013

In 2013 the Christopher Bu atelier cut two douppioni pieces for the actress Fan Bingbing. The first was a stamp-printed umbrella skirt, photographed in Paris with the Eiffel Tower behind her. The second was a spring/summer 2013 plaid ensemble, worn to the Skiptrace press conference in Cannes. Look at how the print sits on the slub, and how the skirts hold their shape. For example, a 19 momme douppioni skirt holds its A-line instead of collapsing, because the slubbed weave resists the drape that flattens a crepe. Both garments appear again on the label's own lookbook models, which confirms them as Christopher Bu product cut from the same douppioni we supply as greige today.
