The division is the easy part. What decides whether the number describes your shipment is what went on the balance, how many pieces of it, and in what air. ASTM D3776 leaves those choices to four named options(https://www.astm.org/d3776-09.html), and its small-swatch option carries a sentence that buyers read past: the results are considered applicable to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot. Two honest reports can still disagree about the same cloth.\n\nA g/m² figure is a mass over an area, so the population the mass came from decides what it describes.\n\nASTM D3776 names its full-piece option for acceptance testing of commercial shipments and limits the small-swatch option to the sample.\n\nConditioning moves the reading by an amount comparable to the tolerance a contract may set, so the air belongs in the report.\n\nA report without the option, the specimen count, the atmosphere and the balance tolerance cannot be checked against your own re-test.\n\nWidth matters twice: once when a figure is converted, and once when the cloth is measured slack rather than under tension(https://www.iso.org/standard/40768.html).\n\nWhat the number on a spec sheet actually measures\nMass per unit area is a mass divided by an area(https://www.iso.org/standard/9335.html), which is why the unit is written as grams over square meters rather than as a single weight. It is not thickness. ISO 5084 describes a method for the determination of the thickness of textiles and textile products under specified pressure, and it is not applicable to textile floor coverings, nonwovens, geotextiles and coated fabrics(https://www.iso.org/standard/23348.html). It is not momme either, though momme is the trade's name for the same quantity; the conversion between the two units is set out in the momme guide.\n\nThe unit also tells you which quantity you hold. ISO 3801 gives the reason grams per meter and grams per square meter are not interchangeable by width alone. For some fabrics the two are related simply by the width, but for others variations in structure may introduce an important distinction between them. Take a figured jacquard, for example, where the ground and the figure take up yarn at different rates. No single width converts one figure into the other.\n\nIts scope is wider than buyers expect: woven fabrics including stretch types, made up full width or folded down the middle, complete pieces as well as sample lengths. The folded state is the part that trips a conversion. Cloth presented folded down the middle and the same cloth opened out are two different widths, so a mass reported against length becomes a mass per unit area only once the width and the presentation are both known.\n\nFour sampling options, and only one of them is a lot result\nOption A is the full-piece route, the one ASTM D3776 ties to trade practice(https://www.astm.org/d3776-09.html). Its text says the option may be used for acceptance testing of commercial shipments since it has been used extensively in the trade. Option C is the small-swatch route, and it runs the other way: the results are considered to be applicable to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot.\n\nTable 1. The four sampling options named in ASTM D3776, as stated in the section text read for this page. \"Not stated\" marks cells the text does not cover.\n\nOption B is named for the full width and Option D for narrow fabrics.
