Slubs Are Not Defects: Agreeing an Acceptable-Irregularity Standard for Dupioni

A slub is not a defect in Dupioni Silk. It is the visible result of a yarn reeled from a double cocoon, and the cloth cannot be woven without one12. A claim begins only where the irregularity leaves a range both sides wrote down. Agree that range before the lot ships.

Key takeaways

  • ›Doupion yarn comes from a double cocoon spun by two worms, and it is coarse, non-uniform and stubby1.
  • ›The London Museum's doppiona sample, dated 1900 to 1935, is noted as thicker in the thread, taking dye well and creasing less4.
  • ›ASTM D3776 limits a small swatch result to the sample, not the lot10.
  • ›The site publishes 16 and 19 momme, 114 and 140 cm, greige or dyed to order, about 5 percent shrinkage.

A slub is a yarn feature before it is a surface

Doupion is the yarn carrying the thick sections2. A slub is a soft, thick, uneven section in a yarn or thread3. Dupioni Silk is the cloth woven from that yarn, with a fine silk warp and a slubbed weft.

When two worms spin one cocoon the filament tangles and will not unwind smoothly, so it is set aside from reeling and used to make a coarse, non-uniform, stubby yarn1. FAO lists crowded mounting conditions, high temperatures, high humidity and mutation among the causes, and files double cocoons as defective1. That heading is where arguments start. Defective there means the cocoon will not run as a reeling input, which says nothing about the cloth.

The CAMEO doupion entry, from the Museum of Fine Arts Boston materials database, describes an irregular thread formed from two or more cocoons, with uneven thick and thin areas2. It also lists the spellings: douppioni, dupion, dupioni, doupin2. Name the one the mill certificate will use.

What the cloth looks like, and what a photograph cannot carry

The thick places run across the width, short and spaced without rhythm, and light breaks along them. Between them the fine warp shows as a flatter line. That contrast is what a buyer is often asked to approve, though a photograph carries no momme, picks, width or roll identity.

Construction explains the crisp hand. The plain weave pairs a fine silk warp with the doupion weft, so the cloth stands off flat and holds a fold. The London Museum record for its doppiona sample notes a slightly thicker thread containing irregularities, and adds that the textile dyes well and is less prone to creasing4.

Why the irregularity cannot be woven out

The causes listed in the FAO reeling manual are cocoon-house conditions, not faults a loom can correct1. A mill can sort cocoons and refuse a batch. It cannot weave a doupion yarn even.

A filament is two fibroin strands held together by sericin, and the gum is removed by boiling the fibres in soapy water or a dilute alkali5. Raw silk contains 20 to 30 percent sericin6. That gum leaves the cloth during degumming. The published weight describes the cloth as supplied, at 16 to 19 momme and roughly 69 to 95 g/m². The sericin figure describes raw silk. The two are not convertible, so a weight clause names the state.

ISO 3801 notes that structural variation may create an important distinction between mass per length and mass per area7. A slubbed cloth varies by construction, so one reading speaks for one place. Where two readings on a bolt disagree, both belong in the record.

Dupioni Silk against the other three names in an order

Three of the four names here describe something other than a plain slubbed cloth.

Habotai Silk

Habotai Silk is the site's lightweight plain-weave mulberry silk, 6 to 16 momme at 114 cm, with a crisp hand and a fine, even surface. It sits in the same weave family with none of the slub. In a pale shade the two can look close on a shelf, so the surface is the field to write down.

Greige Silk Fabric

Greige Silk Fabric is cloth in the state it leaves the loom, unbleached and undyed8. Dharma Trading describes greige goods as washed but neither bleached nor optically whitened9. Both cloths are supplied greige or dyed to order, from a standard shade card or a custom colour. Use the term the mill's own paperwork uses.

Raw Silk Yarn

Raw silk is untreated silk reeled directly from the cocoon, holding 20 to 30 percent sericin6. It names a yarn, not a cloth. An order asking for raw silk where greige cloth is meant leaves the mill guessing which product to quote.

What a swatch certifies, and what it does not

ASTM D3776 covers measurement of fabric mass per unit area and sets out four options10. Option A takes a full piece, roll, bolt or cut, and the method names it for acceptance testing. Option C takes a small swatch, and there the results apply to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot10. The swatch on the table certifies the swatch on the table.

ISO 5077 sets out a method for dimensional change under specified washing and drying12; ISO 105-C06 sets out methods for colour resistance to laundering13. Neither fixes a pass level, only how the test runs.

Where an accepted slub becomes a claim

A slub accepted on the flat can still fail in the seam. Threads Magazine reports that double-cocoon silk is weaker than regular silk because of the irregularity of the yarn11, and seam slippage is one place a workroom meets that weakness. Approve a sewn seam sample with the flat sample, and decide who carries the cost of a resewn panel. The range the cloth is judged against is written down in where the slub sets the tolerance.

Certificates and scope

OEKO-TEX publishes a label check where a number is entered and verified, case sensitive14. STANDARD 100 covers textiles tested for harmful substances from yarn to finished product, in classes, with Class II covering direct skin contact15. A certificate against a yarn is not one against a finished cloth.

GOTS works through documented tracking of certified materials, using transaction certificates16. A transaction certificate names a shipment, which makes it the document to request when a number is offered for a lot.

Where a contract cites a national product standard, name the edition and the clause. GB/T 15551-2016, Mulberry silk fabrics, is current, published on 25 April 2016 and implemented on 1 November 201618.

The clauses to write

Merchant pages show the trade's own wording. Dharma Trading states that small slubs are natural in silk and not defects, and that its fabrics are not pre-shrunk9. Silk Designs states a minimum order of one roll, roughly 30 to 35 m17.

To those, add the one thing no merchant page can supply: a limit, if you want one, written as a sampling rule two inspectors can apply to the same piece. The published product page carries the fields this article treats as fixed: 16 and 19 momme, 114 and 140 cm, greige or dyed to order, and 5 percent shrinkage.

Are slubs in Dupioni Silk a defect?

No. They come from a yarn reeled from double cocoons, and the coarse yarn is why the cloth exists in this form12. Merchant pages keep small irregularities outside the defect list9. A claim needs a departure from a written range.

How many slubs per metre should I accept?

No source used here publishes a numeric limit for dupioni, so any figure is the buyer's own. Set it as a sampling rule tied to the sealed sample. For instance, a rule asking an inspector to compare one metre against that sample can be repeated; a rule asking for few slubs cannot.

Does a passing swatch result apply to the bulk lot?

No. ASTM D3776 states that results apply to the sample only and not necessarily to the lot10. The swatch decides appearance; the lot is a separate measurement decision.

Should the contract name greige or finished weight?

Name the state. Greige is unbleached and undyed as it leaves the loom8. Dupioni Silk is supplied greige or dyed to order, with 21 to 22 momme on request beyond the published 16 and 19. A weight figure without a state allows two readings of one number.

Does a certificate number let me accept a lot?

It lets you check a scope. Enter the number in the issuer's label check14, read the product class and the standard cited15, then ask for the transaction certificate naming the shipment16.
