Silk fabric types: 22 constructions, and how to choose one

Silk is not one fabric. It is a family of roughly 22 commercial constructions that share a fibre and almost nothing else — a 5 momme habotai lining and a 30 momme mikado ball gown are the same protein and behave like different materials. This page sets out each construction by weave, weight and use, states which nine we supply as standing products from Yizhou, and is explicit about the ones we only quote after confirming mill capacity.

Key takeaways

  • Silk is a family of about 22 commercial constructions, not one fabric: weight runs from 5 momme habotai lining to 30 momme mikado.
  • Three numbers decide behaviour: weave, weight in momme, and finish or colour route. The trade name is shorthand for those three.
  • 1 momme is 4.34 g/m². Degumming between greige and finished cloth removes roughly a quarter of the weight, so a greige figure overstates the finished fabric.
  • Nine constructions are standing products with their own specification pages; the rest are woven or quoted after capacity is confirmed.
  • Five constructions in the list are quoted only after a mill check, and this page deliberately publishes no minimum and no price for them.

Three axes define any silk fabric

Buyers new to silk tend to shop by name — “I need dupioni” — and experienced buyers shop by three numbers, because the name is only shorthand for them.

01

Weave

Plain, twill, satin, jacquard, pile. This decides lustre, drape direction and whether the cloth can hold an edge. Dupioni and habotai are both plain weave. One is slubbed and stiff, the other smooth and limp, because of the yarn and the finishing, not the interlacing.

02

Weight in momme

1 momme is 4.34 g/m². Weight decides whether the cloth falls, holds or collapses. It is the single number most often misreported, because it can be measured on greige or on finished fabric. Degumming takes away weight in between.

03

Finish and colour route

Greige, dyed to a standard shade, dyed to a custom match, or woven with the colour in the yarn. The same base cloth can differ in price by a wide margin across these routes. The route has to be fixed before a price means anything.

The three terms this page keeps using

Momme is the weight unit the silk trade quotes, and one momme is 4.34 g/m² of cloth. Greige is the undyed cloth as it leaves the loom, before finishing, dyeing or any change in weight. Dupioni is a plain-weave silk woven from slubbed yarn, which is why its surface is irregular where a charmeuse is smooth.

The gap between a greige figure and a finished figure is not a rounding error. Degumming, the process that removes sericin from the yarn, takes 22–28% of the greige weight (Standardization Administration of China, 2008). A cloth sold as 22 momme on greige therefore lands near 16 to 17 momme finished, and the finished figure is the one that decides how a garment hangs.

Choosing by what you are cutting

The fastest route through 22 constructions is to start from the garment rather than the fabric name. Four routes cover most enquiries we receive. For example, a bridal atelier asking for structure is routed to mikado or duchesse, while a lining buyer asking for slip is routed to habotai at 5 to 8 momme.

You are cutting a gown that has to hold its shape

Start at 19 momme and go up. Dupioni at 19–22, satin at 22–30, or jacquard over a structured base. Below 16 momme the cloth follows the body instead of shaping it — which is correct for a bias slip and wrong for a corseted bodice.

Dupioni →Satin →

You are cutting bridal

Two different problems. The outer layer needs body and a clean edge — dupioni, satin, jacquard. The underlayers need to disappear — habotai at 5–8 momme, or georgette where you want a softer fall. Most bridal programmes buy both from the same supplier so the dye lots match.

Habotai →Dupioni →

You are cutting modestwear or abaya

Weight is not the priority; drape and opacity are. Georgette at 10–16 momme and stretch satin at 14–28 cover most of the category — georgette for layered volume, stretch satin where the garment has to move with the body and still fall closed.

You are cutting qipao, hanfu or ceremonial dress

The cloth has to carry a motif. Jacquard does the work that embroidery would otherwise do, at a fraction of the labour, and reads correctly under stage and camera light. Satin and georgette handle the plain panels around it.

What this page does not claim

We do not stock all 22 constructions, and a table that implied otherwise would waste your time at the sampling stage. Five constructions in the list — mikado, gazar, radzimir, faille and peau de soie — are quoted only after we confirm capacity, and we deliberately publish no minimum and no price for them here.

We also do not publish a price per meter on this page. Silk is quoted against a specification: grade, momme measured on finished cloth, width, colour route and quantity. A single headline number would be true for one combination and misleading for the rest. The pricing logic is set out separately in the silk fabric price guide.

Questions buyers ask before sampling

How many types of silk fabric are there?

There is no fixed number, because a type is defined by three independent things: the weave, the weight and the finish. In commercial practice most mills work with roughly 20 to 30 named constructions. This page covers 22 of them, from habotai at 5 momme to peau de soie at the heavy end. The same yarn can become a chiffon or a mikado depending on how it is woven and finished.

What is the difference between silk fabric types and silk grades?

Grade describes the fibre — 4A, 5A and 6A refer to evenness, cleanness and neatness of the raw silk filament. Type describes the cloth — how those filaments were woven. A 6A yarn woven into a loose chiffon is still a 6A yarn, and a low-grade yarn woven into a heavy satin is still low grade. Buyers who only ask about momme, or only ask about grade, are each holding half the specification.

Which silk fabric is best for evening gowns?

For a gown that has to hold a sculpted line, 19 momme and above: dupioni, heavy satin, or a jacquard over a structured base. For a bias-cut gown that should move, 16–19 momme satin or charmeuse. The failure mode in both directions is the same — cloth chosen for how it looks flat on a table rather than how it behaves on a body under light.

Can you supply silk fabric in small quantities?

Mini fabric samples can be supplied without a material charge once availability is confirmed; the buyer bears courier, customs and related international shipping costs. Full-size fabric and finished garments are chargeable. Bulk minimums depend on the construction, the width and whether the colour is a standard shade or a custom match, and are confirmed in the written quotation rather than quoted as a single number here.

Do you sell silk fabric by the yard or by the meter?

We quote by the meter for export orders, and the product pages state the width the price refers to. Buyers working in yards should confirm the conversion at quotation stage rather than assuming a 1:1 relationship — a 114 cm width and a 140 cm width are not interchangeable when you are costing a marker.

Are gazar, mikado, radzimir, faille and peau de soie available?

These are woven to order through the local mills we work with, and we quote them only after confirming the construction, width and colour against current mill capacity. We do not hold them as stock items, and this page does not state a minimum or a price for them. Ask for a sample and we will tell you honestly whether the construction is available for your quantity.

Is your silk fabric dyed to order?

Yes, on both tracks: standard shade card colours, and custom colour matching to a submitted sample or a specified reference. Greige fabric is also supplied undyed for buyers running their own dyehouse. Colour is what we quote for; surface effects beyond dyeing are a separate conversation and we will say plainly whether they are available.

Sources and method

Methodology: the weight ranges on this page are commercial ranges agreed with the mills, not a mill guarantee for every roll. The finished-weight figure is the one quoted in a written specification. Denier and grade follow GB/T 1797-2008, the national raw silk standard. The origin statements rest on the Yizhou district government and Hechi civil affairs publications. Red-carpet descriptions follow the contemporaneous coverage listed above. The silk attribution for those garments rests on the atelier's own production records. Availability states are checked against the product pages on this site. A construction marked sample on request is not a stock claim.

  1. Standardization Administration of China: GB/T 1797-2008 生丝 (Raw silk) (2008). The national standard that defines raw silk grades and the denier notation used in the weight discussion on this page.
